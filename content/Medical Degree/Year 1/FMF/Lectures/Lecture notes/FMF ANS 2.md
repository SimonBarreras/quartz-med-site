## 1. Sympathetic vs parasympathetic: functions
- **Bladder:** the **parasympathetic contracts the detrusor** muscle to empty the bladder; the **sympathetic inhibits** that contraction, letting the bladder relax and fill, and **closes the sphincters**.
- Generally the sympathetic **closes the sphincters** (anal, urethral, all of them): it goes to the sphincter, not the whole bladder or gut wall.
- **Sympathetic: alertness, "fight or flight"**. **Parasympathetic: brings things back to normal, "rest and digest"**, calm; near vision (pupil constriction).
- **Secretions** for digestion (saliva, gastric, pancreatic) are produced or enhanced by the autonomic system (transcript garbled about which division **[verify]**; it fits "rest and digest", i.e. parasympathetic).
- **Adrenaline** release in a danger situation: **sympathetic**.
- Recap: autonomic ganglion cells (sympathetic and parasympathetic) come from the **neural crest**; second (motor) neurons are always in ganglia.

## 2. Sympathetic ganglia: two sets
- Preganglionic neurons: **lateral horn, T1 to L2** (intermediolateral column).
- **Sympathetic chain (trunk):** **paired** ganglia on both sides of the vertebral column, at each spinal level.
- **Prevertebral (preaortic) ganglia:** **single** ganglia in the abdominal (and pelvic) cavity, **anterior to the vertebral bodies and the abdominal aorta** and its branches; some have names.

## 3. How many chain ganglia?
- Class exercise: spinal levels are 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral (and coccygeal; the class was unsure of the count).
- **Cervical:** instead of one per level, they **fuse into three**: **superior**, **middle** and **inferior cervical** ganglia.
- The inferior cervical also **fuses with the first thoracic** (C7 + T1), with a different name ("you don't need to know"; name not given **[verify]**).
- The rest: **one per level**, including sacral ganglia (more ganglia lower down for the limbs); at the bottom a single unpaired ganglion where the two chains meet.
- [Lucía] Above T1 and below L2 there is no lateral horn: preganglionic fibres from T1 go **up** the chain, those from L2 go **down**, to reach ganglia at those levels.

## 4. Sympathetic routes
### To the skin (body wall)
- Why are chain neurons right next to the spinal nerves? Two small bridges link each spinal nerve to its chain ganglion:
  - one carries **preganglionic** fibres from the spinal nerve **into** the chain;
  - the other carries **postganglionic** fibres **back to the spinal nerve**.
- Postganglionic fibres then **travel with the spinal nerves** to reach small smooth-muscle "viscera" of the **dermis**, which is **segmentally** organized: **arrector pili** muscles (hair follicles), **sweat glands**, blood vessels. Towards the back via the **dorsal ramus**, towards the front via the **ventral ramus**.
- Reaches skin everywhere (except palms and soles, transcript **[verify]**). These structures have **only sympathetic** innervation: fear makes hairs stand up.
- [Lucía] White communicating ramus (preganglionic, myelinated) and grey communicating ramus (postganglionic, unmyelinated).

### To the thoracic viscera
- Preganglionic neurons at the same level exit the spinal nerve and enter the chain; for higher levels (cervical ganglia, e.g. supplying the **heart**, which originates from cervical levels), preganglionic fibres from **T1 to T4** enter the chain and **go up**.
- Postganglionic fibres leave the ganglia **towards the midline**, forming a mesh around the **bronchi, lungs, heart, oesophagus**.
- These are the **sympathetic cardiac nerves**: **postganglionic** fibres.

### To the abdominal and pelvic viscera
- Preganglionic neurons are in the lateral horn (thoracic levels), but the second neurons are in the **prevertebral ganglia** in the abdomen and pelvis.
- The preganglionic fibre leaves the spinal nerve, **passes through** the chain ganglion **without synapsing** (no neurons there for it), **crosses the diaphragm**, and synapses in a prevertebral ganglion near the aorta (e.g. at the superior mesenteric artery). Postganglionic fibres go to the gut, liver, etc.
- These preganglionic fibres form the **splanchnic nerves** (thoracic splanchnic; also lumbar, going to pelvic ganglia).
- **Contrast:** cardiac nerves = **postganglionic**; splanchnic nerves = **preganglionic**.

## 5. Blood vessels: sympathetic only
- How does the sympathetic shut down the GI tract in fight or flight? Not by acting on gut muscle (that is the parasympathetic's job): by reducing its **blood supply**.
- The **sympathetic is the only system innervating blood vessels**, causing **vasoconstriction**: viscera with less blood reduce their activity. Skin too: in fight or flight you look **pale** (cutaneous vasoconstriction).
- Muscle blood vessels **dilate** in fight or flight, also sympathetic: the same transmitter acts through **different receptors** in different tissues (pupils, bronchi, heart, skin vessels, muscle vessels). "Blood vessels of the muscle have cholinergic receptors" **[check]**: [Lucía] says the sympathetic postganglionic fibres to skeletal muscle vessels (and sweat glands) **release acetylcholine**.
- Student question on brain blood flow: left open ("let's do some research").
- [Lucía] Sympathetic nerves enter the viscera with the arteries; the vascular system, kidney and glycaemia regulation are exclusively sympathetic.

## 6. Parasympathetic system
- Preganglionic neurons in the **brainstem** and at **S2 to S4**.
- Sacral levels have **no lateral horn**: the preganglionic neurons are in the **ventral horn** region (motor), exit by the ventral root, leave the spinal nerve and go to the midline viscera (e.g. bladder).
- **Ganglia are in the walls of the viscera**, often **microscopic**: **long preganglionic**, **very short postganglionic** fibres. (Sympathetic: visible postganglionic nerves.)
- **Pelvic splanchnic nerves**: the preganglionic parasympathetic fibres from S2 to S4 (transcript "Felix-Platlick"). Not to be confused with the sympathetic splanchnic nerves.
- **Vagus nerve** (10th cranial): preganglionic parasympathetic fibres from the brainstem, a very thick nerve leaving the skull; branches to **thoracic** viscera (heart...) and **abdominal** viscera: stomach, small intestine, ascending colon and the **proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon**.
- **Pelvic splanchnic** nerves go **up** to the **distal third of the transverse colon**, descending colon, **rectum**, and to the **bladder** etc.
- In the GI tract the parasympathetic ganglia lie **within the gut layers**, forming **two plexuses**: one **between the two muscle layers**, another **between the mucosa and the first muscle layer** (part of a very complex system, coordinating contraction along the gut). Sympathetic supply to the gut: essentially just the blood vessels.
- [Lucía] Vagus leaves via the jugular foramen; slows heart rate, contracts bronchi, stimulates the GI tract. Pelvic splanchnic nerves also reach prostate/clitoris (erection) and form the inferior hypogastric plexus.

## 7. Neurotransmitters and the adrenal medulla
| Fibre | Neurotransmitter |
|---|---|
| **All preganglionic** (sympathetic and parasympathetic), synapse in the ganglion | **Acetylcholine**, always |
| **Sympathetic postganglionic** (chain and prevertebral ganglia) | **Norepinephrine** |
| Exceptions: sympathetic fibres to **sweat glands** and the dilating vessels of **skeletal muscle** | **Acetylcholine** |
| Parasympathetic postganglionic | Acetylcholine ([Lucía]; not explicit in the transcript) |

- **Adrenal medulla:** some neural crest cells migrate into the adrenal gland and form something like a **sympathetic ganglion**. When they receive **preganglionic** fibres, they release **norepinephrine and epinephrine into the bloodstream**: a dual nervous and **hormonal** action; very fast; the whole body is activated.
- [Lucía] These are **chromaffin cells**; the adrenal medulla secretes mostly **epinephrine** (80%, 20% norepinephrine). Preganglionic = cholinergic; sympathetic postganglionic = adrenergic; parasympathetic postganglionic = cholinergic.

## 8. Visceral sensory fibres
- Sensory neurons from the viscera have their cell bodies in the **dorsal root ganglia**, together with the somatosensory neurons from skin and dermis: peripheral process, cell body in the ganglion, central process ending in the **dorsal horn**.
- **Key question:** with which system does sensory information travel?
  - **Distension** (awareness that the stomach, bladder or rectum is full) travels **with the parasympathetic** fibres, in the opposite direction. In the CNS it arrives right next to the parasympathetic motor neurons, so if it is appropriate the reflex (e.g. going to the toilet) is close at hand.
  - **Pain** travels **with the sympathetic** fibres, in the opposite direction (e.g. stomach pain from an ulcer enters via the chain).
- [Lucía] Exception: **pelvic pain** travels with the pelvic splanchnic (parasympathetic) nerves. Pain reaches the spinal nerve via the white communicating ramus. Distension from viscera down to the distal third of the transverse colon travels with the vagus.

## 9. Not recorded this year: the ANS TBL case ([Lucía])
- Epidural anaesthesia in labour: blood pressure drops (130/85 to 80/45 mmHg) with tachycardia (120 bpm): the anaesthetic blocked sympathetic preganglionic fibres, removing the **basal sympathetic tone** on vascular smooth muscle (alpha-1 receptors), so arterioles dilate.
- Treatment: fluids, then **alpha-1 agonists** (ephedrine, phenylephrine). Possible reflex **bradycardia** via the baroreceptor reflex (carotid sinus, glossopharyngeal nerve, solitary nucleus, dorsal vagal nucleus, vagus, M2 receptors); treat with **muscarinic antagonists** (atropine).
- Key ideas: vessels have only sympathetic innervation and depend on basal tone; the heart has both.
