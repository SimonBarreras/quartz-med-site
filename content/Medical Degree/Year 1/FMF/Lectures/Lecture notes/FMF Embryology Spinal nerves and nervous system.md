## 1. Recap: the somatic peripheral nervous system
- Information comes from the periphery (skin, muscle...) to the spinal cord (for trunk and limbs); the CNS integrates it and sends a motor response back through the peripheral system to the target organs.
- **Spinal nerves** innervate the **somatic derivatives**, which come from the **somites**:
  - striated muscle from the **dorsomedial** and **ventrolateral myotome** (transcript "metrolatic myotone");
  - skin (dermis) from the **dermatome**.
- **Smooth muscle and cardiac muscle** come instead from the **lateral mesoderm**, specifically its **visceral (splanchnic) layer**, which surrounds the organs; the **somatic (parietal) layer** lines the body wall.
- Clip 1 fragment: the lecturer points out a whitish staining, a central process going towards the spinal cord, and the somites. **[verify]** context.
- [Lucía] Dorsomedial (epaxial) myotome gives the intrinsic back muscles; ventrolateral (hypaxial) myotome gives the anterolateral body wall muscles (intercostals, rectus). Smooth muscle of the gut wall from the splanchnic layer of lateral mesoderm; the gut mucosa from endoderm.

## 2. Smooth and cardiac vs striated muscle
- Question to the class: what do smooth and cardiac muscle have in common that differs from striated muscle? Student answer, confirmed ("that's exactly it"): their contraction is **involuntary**; skeletal (striated) muscle is **voluntary**.
- So the peripheral nervous system has two components:
  - **somatic**: spinal nerves and plexuses, innervating skin and striated muscle;
  - **autonomic**: a different series of nerves innervating smooth muscle, glands (to contract the gland and release secretions) and sensory structures in the mucosa of the viscera. "Autonomic" refers to their being **involuntary**.
- Plan: motor fibres today; **sensory** information "the next day".
- [Lucía] Somatic plexuses = striated muscle; visceral plexuses = muscle in viscera and glands. The ANS has sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions.

## 3. Where the neurons come from
- Spinal cord recap: **alar plate** (sensory area) and **basal plate** (motor), separated by the **sulcus limitans**.
- **Neural crest cells** migrate out:
  - some form the **sensory** neurons: **pseudounipolar** cells (in the dorsal root ganglia);
  - others form **autonomic ganglia**: these motor ganglion cells also originate from neural crest cells.
- Autonomic ganglia formed by migrating neural crest cells (board drawing, e.g. fibres going to the heart):
  - **paired** ganglia at every level on both sides of the spinal cord (a chain);
  - **single (unpaired)** clusters in front, "normally in the abdomen". **[verify]** wording garbled.
- [Lucía] Neural crest cells (ectodermal, becoming mesenchymal; "4th germ layer") give rise to sensory neurons (dorsal root ganglia), melanocytes, autonomic nervous system cells and Schwann cells. They give rise to both sympathetic and parasympathetic ganglionic neurons. Pseudounipolar: one process leaves the body and splits into a central and a peripheral process.
- [Lucía] Sympathetic ganglia: (1) **sympathetic trunk/chain** (paravertebral), paired at each level; (2) **preaortic single ganglia** (prevertebral), ventral to the aorta, in the abdominal and pelvic cavities.

## 4. Central control without consciousness
- Autonomic does not mean independent: some ganglia work "pretty much" on their own, but there is **central control**; the difference is that it is **not conscious**.
- Example: while lecturing, the lecturer needs airways dilated, muscles well irrigated, heart quite active. Sitting in the office with a coffee after class, heart rate goes down and blood goes to the digestive system. Depending on the activity, the **CNS controls these neurons** without conscious control.
- [Lucía] Sympathetic = "fight or flight" (activates organs for a challenge: pupils dilate, heart rate up, digestion inhibited); parasympathetic = "rest and digest" (digestion stimulated, less blood to skeletal muscle, pupils constrict).

## 5. A two-neuron motor system
- The ganglion cells are controlled by **motor cells from the basal plate** of the spinal cord, placed more laterally than the somatic motor neurons.
- **Preganglionic neuron**: in the CNS; its axon goes to the ganglion and connects with the ganglion cell. It receives input from higher up in the CNS (the brain).
- **Postganglionic (ganglionic) neuron**: in the ganglion; it is the effector, targeting the visceral cells (e.g. smooth muscle of the gut).
- Compare: **somatic** system = **one** neuron in the ventral horn going directly to striated muscle; **autonomic** = **two** neurons, one in the CNS and one in a ganglion.
- The preganglionic neuron is a **visceral efferent** (vs somatic efferent) motor neuron.
- Naming: preganglionic neuron/fibre (before the ganglion), ganglion, postganglionic fibre.
- [Lucía] The second neuron is always in a ganglion, outside the CNS; the first is in the spinal cord or brainstem. Its axon is the preganglionic fibre.

## 6. Location and route of the sympathetic preganglionic neurons
- The visceral motor cell bodies cluster in the **lateral horn** of the spinal cord.
- The lateral horn is a **column** of neurons from **T1 to L2** (12 thoracic levels plus L1 and L2); not at cervical or sacral levels. All the **sympathetic preganglionic** neurons are there.
- Question: how does the preganglionic axon leave the spinal cord? Being motor, by the **ventral root** with the other motor axons.
- The **sympathetic chain** runs on either side of the vertebral column on the posterior wall of the thoracic and abdominal cavities. Its ganglia have nothing to do with the dorsal root ganglia.
- The preganglionic fibre goes out with the spinal nerve, then **leaves it and enters the chain**, where it synapses on a ganglion neuron; that neuron's axon travels to the target (e.g. the heart). The lecturer named the two connecting branches (transcript "bone-councing fiber, and the free-councing") **[verify]**; [Lucía]: preganglionic fibres (myelinated, whitish) form the **white communicating ramus**; postganglionic fibres (unmyelinated) form the **grey communicating ramus**.
- **The recording ends here** (break).
- [Lucía] Because the lateral horn exists only at T1 to L2, preganglionic axons ascend or descend in the chain to reach ganglia above and below. Targets: dermis (sweat glands, arrector pili, blood vessels) only sympathetic, via chain ganglia and the dorsal or ventral ramus; thoracic viscera via chain ganglia (postganglionic cardiac nerves); abdominal and pelvic viscera via preaortic ganglia (preganglionic **splanchnic nerves**). Sympathetic preganglionic fibres are short, postganglionic long. Parasympathetic preganglionic neurons are in the brainstem (mainly vagus) and sacral cord (S2 to S4).
