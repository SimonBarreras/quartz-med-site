## 1. Epithelium
### General characteristics (four)
1. **Seated on a basement membrane** (all epithelia, stratified and simple). The basement membrane connects the epithelial cells with the surrounding (connective) tissue. The interface is formed by fibres from the connective tissue side and something from the epithelial side (transcript: the interface "is called the basal ring", the epithelial component "anidesmosol" **[verify]** both terms).
2. **Regeneration from basal cells:** the cells touching the basement membrane are **basal cells**; easy to see in stratified epithelium, present but hard to see in simple epithelium. Regeneration starts from proliferation of basal cells: if proliferation stops, the epithelium cannot regenerate and stays damaged.
3. **Polarity:** every epithelial cell has an **apical, a basal and a lateral** part. On the image the apical part shows a dark line (transcript garbled, "micro-mining... is called" **[verify]** which apical structure he named).
4. **Avascular:** no blood vessels in the epithelium; all vessels are in the connective tissue. Lateral attachments between cells (transcript garbled for the first one **[verify]**) and **desmosomes**, which leave spaces between cells: important for **selective permeation** of molecules, needed because the tissue is avascular.
- A **blood vessel** is a channel lined by **simple squamous epithelium = endothelium**.

## 2. Glands
- **Goblet cell:** an epithelial cell of glandular type that secretes **mucus**: a **unicellular gland**. It is **exocrine**, yet has **no duct**: as a single cell, it secretes directly onto the surface of the organ.
- **Exocrine vs endocrine** (two gland types seen): **exocrine glands have ducts**; **endocrine glands have no ducts**, all secretion goes into the circulation.
- Multicellular exocrine glands: round secretory portions = **acini** (transcript "acidase"), always connected to a **duct**. Ducts and acini are epithelial, so they have all four characteristics and are always surrounded by a basement membrane.
- **Acinar cells: mucous or serous.**

| | Mucous acinus | Serous acinus |
|---|---|---|
| Staining | **Light** | **Dark** |
| Secretion | Mucus | Watery (his example: saliva, with enzymes) |
| Why | **One large vesicle** of mucus at the top of the cell; the membrane only surrounds it | **Hundreds of small vesicles**, each bounded by membrane (same elements as the plasma membrane), packed together: dark |

- He also said mucus "is rich in protein" (passage garbled **[verify]**). The explanation of the colour is the storage, not the content.
- **Mixed glands:** mucous and serous together.
- Student question: do the secretion modes (merocrine, apocrine, holocrine; transcript "botanization, applecine, neurocine") apply to endocrine glands? **No, always exocrine**: endocrine secretion goes directly to the circulation; in exocrine glands the secretion goes from the cell through the duct to the surface.

## 3. Connective tissue
- **Function:** to connect, to give support.
- **Three components in every type:** **cells** (fibroblasts), **fibres** (in general collagen) and **ground substance** (proteoglycans). The special types add their own details.
- **Families:** **embryonic** and **adult**; adult divided into **proper** and **special** connective tissue (he first said two families, then three).
- **Collagen synthesis** is split: collagen is a very large structure and the fibroblast is small, so the **intracellular** part makes small collagen molecules, released to the **extracellular** space, where enzymes assemble them into collagen. The striations ("zebra") on collagen fibres come from the way they are assembled.

| Type | Features he gave | Location / example |
|---|---|---|
| **Loose** | Thin collagen fibres; more cellular | Under epithelia |
| **Dense irregular** | Thick collagen fibres, no single orientation | |
| **Dense regular** | Thick collagen fibres; cells and fibres **aligned** in one direction: strength in that direction only. White spaces = ground substance; nuclei = fibroblasts | **Tendons** |
| **Reticular** (special) | Made of a special collagen, **type III** (reticular fibres); gives structure and resistance to organs formed just by cells | **Haematopoietic organs:** spleen, bone marrow, thymus |
| **White (yellow, unilocular) adipose** (special) | One big **adipocyte** with **one large lipid droplet**, nucleus pushed to the periphery | Function: **store energy** |
| **Brown (multilocular) adipose** (special) | Smaller adipocytes with **several small lipid droplets**; nucleus in general central | Function: **produce heat**; found in **babies** |
| **Embryonic mucous** | Cells are **mesenchymal cells**, not fibroblasts; **stellate** shape; lots of proteoglycans; very delicate | Embryo |

- Active vs inactive fibroblasts were pointed out on one image.
- **All connective tissues are vascularized** (because they are connective tissue), but **not equally**: an image with very few vessels; fewer vessels bring less blood to the tissue.
- [Lucía] Collagen types for identification: connective tissue type I; cartilage type II; reticular connective tissue type III; basement membrane type IV.

## 4. Cartilage
- A **special type of connective tissue**: the three components are present. Characteristic fibre: **collagen type II** (always found in cartilage).
- Cells: **chondrocytes**, in spaces called **lacunae**.
- **Territorial matrix** (around the chondrocytes) and **interterritorial matrix** (between them) differ in collagen and proteoglycan concentration. He: more proteoglycans means more water; the **interterritorial matrix** has **more fibres, so it is darker** (the sentence on the territorial matrix is garbled **[verify]**).
  - **[check]** Lucía's "Integration of basic tissues" section: territorial = more fibres, interterritorial = more proteoglycans. Her "Cartilage ECM" section says the opposite: territorial = darker, richer in proteoglycans; interterritorial = lighter, fewer proteoglycans. Her two sections conflict; the lecturer's statement on the interterritorial matrix agrees with neither cleanly.
- **Hyaline cartilage:** found in **articular cartilage** (joints).
- **Perichondrium:** "always" surrounding hyaline cartilage, a layer of **fibrous tissue** (fibroblasts and collagen). Its fibroblasts differentiate into **chondroblasts**, which move to the centre to form chondrocytes. Difference between fibroblast and chondroblast: the chondroblast secretes **cartilaginous matrix** (collagen type II).
  - **[check]** [Lucía]: **articular cartilage has no perichondrium** (a specialized form of hyaline cartilage). [Lucía] Perichondrium: outer fibrous layer and inner chondrogenic layer.
- **Growth:**

| Type | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Appositional** | By layers, from the perichondrium | The most effective |
| **Interstitial** | **Clonal expansion of mature chondrocytes** | Forms **isogenous groups**; a way of cartilage regeneration, but **very slow and ineffective** |

- Other types: **elastic cartilage** (transcript "last cartilage") and **fibrocartilage**, located in the **intervertebral disc**.

## 5. Bone
- Two parts: **cortical** and **trabecular** bone. Bone can also be **mature** or **immature**, and **both can be present in cortical and in trabecular bone**.
  - **[check]** [Lucía] treats trabecular bone as immature bone and cortical bone as mature bone ("In trabecular bone (immature bone), we don't find osteons").
- Mature bone is characterized by **lines = lamellae**: **concentric** in cortical bone, **longitudinal** (lines) in trabecular bone. The lines are visible because the collagen has **different orientations** (and is mineralized).
- **Cortical bone:** functional unit = **osteon**: a central **canal** with the vessels, surrounded by concentric lamellae of bone matrix.
- Between the lamellae: the cells producing bone matrix, the **osteocytes**.
- **Bone remodelling:** bone is a very active tissue; running or going to the gym makes small fractures in bone, which is good because it stimulates remodelling.
  - **Osteocytes** come from **osteoblasts**, which come from "fibroblasts or osteoprogenitor cells".
  - Osteocytes communicate through **prolongations of the cytoplasm** touching each other, not through signalling molecules. [Lucía] These prolongations run in **canaliculi** between lacunae.
  - **Osteoclasts** destroy bone, part of remodelling.
- **Immature vs mature bone:**

| Immature | Mature |
|---|---|
| **Non-lamellar** (transcript name "ocular" **[verify]**; [Lucía] "woven bone") | **Lamellar** |
| Less organized | More organized: osteons (cortical) or lines (trabecular) |
| Can be **non-mineralized**: the first bone formed, by **osteoblasts**; the non-mineralized organic matrix = **osteoid** ("this word is important") | Mineralized |

- Step by step the osteoid becomes mineralized (blue on the image), and mineralization forms the lamellae.
- Trabecular bone image: osteoblasts secreting osteoid, mature bone with lamellae, an osteoclast destroying bone.
- **[check]** [Lucía]: the osteoid "is secreted by osteocytes" (in one sentence); the lecturer says osteoid is formed by **osteoblasts**. Lucía's cell list agrees with him (osteoblasts secrete osteoid and then become osteocytes).

## 6. Muscle
- **Striated** (skeletal and cardiac) and **non-striated** (smooth).
- Striation comes from the contraction unit inside the cells, the **sarcomere**: actin and myosin organized into contractile structures, one sarcomere next to the other; the **lines and bands** arise where sarcomeres connect and where the contractile proteins overlap. Know the names of the lines (exam flag).

| | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit | **Muscle fibre**, bigger than a cell: fusion of several cells | Much smaller fibres | Very small cells |
| Nuclei | **Several**, always at the **periphery** | **One or two, never more** ([Lucía] centrally located) | ([Lucía] central) |
| Sarcomeres / striation | Yes | Yes | **No sarcomeres**; organization "chaotic" |
| Other | | Transcript garbled ("the Indonesian is important to know the concept of education" **[verify]**; [Lucía] describes **intercalated discs** joining cardiac cells) | **Actin and myosin present**; their ends are glued to the **cytoskeleton** by **dense bodies** (the dark dots); their contraction contracts the cytoskeleton, so the whole cell |

## 7. Nervous tissue
- **Neurons:** prolongations = **axons and dendrites**; the cell body has dark dots, **Nissl bodies** = **rough endoplasmic reticulum**, to produce a lot of protein.
- He says the prolongations are "always" protected by **myelin**. **[check]** [Lucía]: fibres can be myelinated or unmyelinated (in the PNS a Schwann cell can surround several axons without myelin).
- **Glial cells** surround the neurons; they cannot be told apart in a regular preparation.

| Glial cell | Function (lecturer) |
|---|---|
| **Microglia** | Clean ("cleaning lady") |
| **Oligodendrocytes** | Produce myelin; interact with neurons |
| **Astrocytes** | Generate the **blood-brain barrier**; interact with blood vessels |

- **Nerves:** outside the CNS, axons form nerves; myelin there is made by **Schwann cells** (he says nerves are "always" surrounded by myelin; see the [check] above). Myelin protects the action potential.
- **Recognizing a nerve on the image:** a **lighter area in the centre** completely surrounded by a **darker layer** (transcript "gillium" **[verify]** which layer he named). Several images of nerves with variations; on one the Schwann cell is not visible but it is still a nerve.
- [Lucía] Connective tissue wrappings of a nerve: epineurium, perineurium, endoneurium. [Slides] question: "What is the value of the connective tissue wrappings found in a nerve?"
- **Ganglion:** in his words "a piece of central nervous system located outside the central nervous system": **neurons** surrounded by small cells, **satellite cells**. Why outside the CNS: to control contraction of specific muscles; e.g. in the **digestive system** nerves reach these neurons, which interpret what the nerve says and what happens around them, far from the CNS, then send an electrical impulse to make the muscle contract.
  - [Lucía] Ganglia: encapsulated aggregations of neuronal cell bodies outside the CNS plus abundant small glial cells (satellite cells).
