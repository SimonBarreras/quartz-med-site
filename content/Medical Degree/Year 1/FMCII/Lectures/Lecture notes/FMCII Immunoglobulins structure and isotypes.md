## 1. History and names
- Vaccination discoveries (original **smallpox** vaccine) more than 200 years ago; about 100 years later, protection after vaccination was found to be transferable with **serum**: due to molecules in a fluid, not cells. Later: the protection is mediated by **antibodies**, produced by **B cells**; then the biochemical and atomic structure; later **hybridoma** technology (next week), used for lab reagents, diagnostic tests and above all **therapeutic antibodies**. [Lucía] Jenner; 1890, serum transfer from immunized animals cured diphtheria.
- **Names** [Slides: antiserum, antitoxin, antibody, gamma-globulin, immunoglobulin]:
  - **Antiserum / antitoxin**: "anti" = against the pathogen, or against a toxin released by it.
  - Serum **electrophoresis** gave alpha, beta, gamma bands; the protective proteins were in the **gamma** band: **gamma-globulins**, then **immunoglobulins**.
  - **Immunoglobulin = antibody.**

## 2. Basic structure
- **Two identical heavy chains + two identical light chains.** [Slides] Linked by **disulfide bonds**.
- **Domains** of about **100-110 amino acids**, each folded into a globular structure by an **intrachain disulfide bond**. **Light chain: 2 domains; heavy chain: 4, or in some cases 5.**
- **Variable domains** (VH, VL) at the **N-terminal** end of both chains: differ between antibodies, contact the antigen: **antigen-binding site**. **Constant domains**: common to antibodies of the same type, mediate the **effector functions**. [Slides] Same.
- **Interchain disulfide bonds**: light chain to heavy chain, and between the two heavy chains.
- **Two forms:**

| | Membrane-bound | Secreted |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Inserted in the B cell membrane | Released into serum and body fluids |
| Function | **Antigen receptor (BCR)**: recognizes antigen through the variable domains, transmits a signal, activates the B cell | **Effector functions** through the Fc region with other cells and molecules |
| Difference | **Short hydrophobic tail** (transmembrane domain) [Slides: 25 aa] | No tail (not transcribed) |

- **Immunoglobulin superfamily:** other molecules share the Ig-like domain fold (e.g. the T cell receptor, transcript "diesel receptor" **[verify]**; **MHC molecules**); they are not antibodies. [Slides] "Some members of the immunoglobulin superfamily" (see the slide).

## 3. Structure from enzymatic digestion [Slides]
- Pure biochemistry on **rabbit IgG** (chosen because it was available in large amounts).

| Enzyme | Fragments | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| **Papain** | **2 Fab** (fragment antigen binding) + **1 Fc** (fragment **crystallizable**) | Fab binds antigen; Fc does not; molar ratio Fab:Fc = 2:1 |
| **Pepsin** | **F(ab')2** (about twice the weight of one Fab, keeps antigen binding); the Fc is degraded into small pieces | |

- Conclusion: one Ig molecule = **two antigen-binding arms + one constant portion** with no antigen recognition.

## 4. Variable domain: hypervariable regions and idiotype
- Protein sequencing of the variable domains: **three regions of high variability** = **hypervariable regions (HV)** = **complementarity-determining regions (CDR)**: they determine complementarity with the antigen. Between them, **framework regions (FR)** that keep the overall structure (FR1-FR4). [Slides] Light chain CDR1-3 at about residues 28-35, 49-59, 92-103; heavy chain 30-36, 49-65, 95-103.
- HV regions: about **7 to 12 amino acids** each.
- **Analogy:** each arm has two "hands" (VL and VH), each with **three fingers** (CDR1-3): **six CDRs** per binding site; the other arm is identical.
- **Idiotype** [Slides]: the unique collection of amino acid sequences within the variable region of a specific antibody; tridimensional structure; **determines antigen specificity**.
- **Isotype vs idiotype:** isotype depends on the **constant** region (IgG, IgM...), i.e. **effector function**; idiotype depends on the **variable** region, i.e. **antigen specificity**. Two IgGs can have different idiotypes.
- [Lucía] Idiotype as a vaccine target in B cell malignancies.

## 5. Hinge region
- Two identical arms can bind two identical epitopes, but the distance between epitopes varies (e.g. monomers of a polysaccharide vs domains of a polymeric protein): the antibody must be **flexible**.
- **Hinge** [Slides]: located **between CH1 and CH2**; rich in **proline** (flexibility of the arms); rich in **cysteine** (interchain disulfide bonds, heavy chain dimerization).

## 6. Classes (isotypes) and polymeric forms
- The constant region is identical within an isotype and differs between isotypes. **Five classes: IgG, IgD, IgE, IgA, IgM** ("class", "type" and "isotype" used as synonyms).
- **Monomers: IgG, IgD, IgE. IgA: dimer. IgM: pentamer** (five monomers).
- **Very important:** as membrane receptor, always a **monomer**, irrespective of isotype; polymeric forms only when **secreted**:

| Isotype | Membrane | Secreted |
|---|---|---|
| IgM | Monomer | **Pentamer** |
| IgG | Monomer | Monomer |
| IgA | Monomer | Monomer in plasma; **dimer** in secretions and mucosae [Slides] |
| IgE, IgD | Monomer | Monomer |

- **IgE and IgM have four constant domains** in the heavy chain; the others three. [Lucía] Same. [Slides table footnote] IgA can exist as monomer, dimer, trimer or tetramer.

## 7. The five effector functions
- Antibodies are made by **adaptive** immunity (needs time, antigen-specific) and connect with **innate** cells and molecules ("integration and coordination").

| Function | Mechanism | Main isotype |
|---|---|---|
| **Opsonization** | Antibodies coat a microbe; **Fc receptors** on **macrophages and neutrophils** bind them: **phagocytosis** plus activation of the phagocyte (it kills what it ate). For **extracellular** microorganisms | IgG [Slides: most IgG subclasses] |
| **Complement activation** | Some antibodies bind complement proteins (serum) and trigger a cascade: inactivation of the pathogen or **lysis** of the cell | **IgM** and most IgG subclasses [Slides] |
| **ADCC** (antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity) | Infected cell shows viral proteins; antibodies bind them; **NK cells** recognize the antibodies through **Fc receptors** and kill the cell. For something **inside** a cell | IgG |
| **Transcytosis** | Ig crosses the epithelium to the mucosal surface; binds the **poly-Ig receptor**; part of the receptor stays bound as the **secretory component** | **IgA** [Slides: IgA and IgM]; respiratory, gastrointestinal, urogenital tracts, **breast milk** |
| **Activation of granulocytes** | **FcεR** on granulocytes binds IgE bound to antigen: **degranulation** (histamine): allergy, atopy; also defence against parasites | **IgE** [Slides: mast cells, eosinophils, basophils] |

- Innate cells gain antigen specificity through antibodies: a macrophage "eats everything", but antibody-marked microbes are targeted.
- Student questions on transcytosis: the vesicle is "kind of an endosome, not exactly"; the poly-Ig receptor is not recycled: it is clipped, a portion leaves bound to IgA (secretory component), and new receptor is produced continuously.

## 8. Isotypes and their functions

| Isotype | Lecturer | [Slides] |
|---|---|---|
| **IgM** | **First** produced chronologically: first Ig on a newly developed (naive) B cell; **primary responses**. Secreted **pentamer: 10 antigen-binding sites**, good for **polymeric antigens** (repeated). Low affinity per site, but **high avidity**. **Complement activation.** In **blood** | Same; avidity = overall interaction between a multivalent Ag and its Ab, increases geometrically |
| **IgG** | **Most abundant**; blood and **tissues**. Subclasses **IgG1-IgG4** with a gradient of properties. **IgG1, 2, 4 cross the placenta** (fetus, neonate). Complement: **IgG3 best**, **IgG4 inactive**. Fc receptors: opsonization, **ADCC**; **neutralization** | Complement IgG3 > IgG1 > IgG2; IgG4 inactive. IgG1 and 3: high-affinity binding to FcR (phagocytes: opsonization; killer cells: ADCC). Neutralization of toxins, bacteria, viruses |
| **IgA** | Plasma monomer, secreted **dimer** in mucosae and tissue. **Mucosal surfaces** "inside but facing outside" (digestive tract): about **400 m²**. 4 binding sites: **high avidity**; **neutralization**; from the mother in **milk** | Predominant Ig in external secretions (breast milk, saliva, bronchial, genitourinary, digestive mucus). Mucosa 400 m² vs **skin 1.8 m²** |
| **IgE** | Fc receptor specific for IgE on granulocytes: release of granules (**histamine**): **allergy, hypersensitivity**; also defence against **parasitic worms (helminths)** that cannot be phagocytosed or killed by ADCC | Mediator of **immediate hypersensitivity**; hay fever, asthma, anaphylactic shock; FcR on **eosinophils** |
| **IgD** | Least known; very low concentration; used as a **marker**: a B cell is **mature** when it expresses **IgD in addition to IgM** | Almost undetectable in serum; on mature B cells (with IgM) |

- **Fc receptors:** each is specific for one isotype; some are **activating**, some **inhibitory**; IgG subclasses differ in affinity for them (subtle amino acid differences).
- **Summary table** [Slides, Table 4-2]: IgG most abundant (IgG1 9 mg/ml) and longest **half-life** (23 days for IgG1, 2, 4; IgG3 8). IgM activates complement; IgG crosses the placenta; IgG in phagocytosis; IgA mucosal transport; IgE degranulation. IgM molecular weight 900,000 (pentamer). IgM is the first isotype of the neonate and of the primary response. See the slide for the full table.

## 9. Biological significance of idiotypes and isotypes
- **Many idiotypes:** one antibody for each of millions of possible epitopes; **each B lymphocyte produces antibodies with one unique idiotype** (specificity). [Slides] Same.
- **Different isotypes:** different **functions** and **distribution** (blood, mucosae, tissues), although all work alike as BCR. A pathogen to be phagocytosed needs opsonization; one living inside cells needs ADCC (both IgG); one entering through the respiratory tract needs IgA.
- **Isotype switching:** a B cell first makes **IgM** (blood); later the **same specificity** (same variable region, same idiotype) with a **different Fc**: IgG, IgA or IgE. The response is tailored to the infection.
