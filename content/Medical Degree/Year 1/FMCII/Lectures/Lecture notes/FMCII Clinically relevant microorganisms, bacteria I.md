## 1. Microorganisms [Slides p2]
- Life forms too small to be seen by the human eye; need **optical or electron microscopes**.
- **Most are harmless**, many **helpful** (food: beer, bread, cheese, fermentation; antibiotics such as penicillin are produced by microorganisms); a **small fraction are pathogens** (the focus of the course).
- Diverse in form and function; in **every environment** (water, food, air, deep sea, deserts). Mostly single cells; some multicellular. [Slides] Live in microbial communities.
- **Map of the course:**

| Cellular | | Acellular |
|---|---|---|
| **Prokaryotes** (unicellular): **bacteria** | **Eukaryotes**: **fungi** (yeast, unicellular; mold; dimorphic), **parasites** (protozoa, unicellular; helminths and arthropods, multicellular; arthropods = vectors) | **Viruses**, viroids/virusoids: **particles**, not cells, not living, much smaller |

## 2. Prokaryotes vs eukaryotes [Slides p3]

| | Prokaryotes (bacteria) | Eukaryotes |
|---|---|---|
| Size | **0.5-3 µm** (up to 10 µm); viruses in the nanometre range | **> 5 µm** [Slides]; lecturer: about 10 times bigger |
| Genome | **Single chromosome** (transcript "single, singular": possibly "circular" **[verify]**), **no nuclear membrane**; **plasmids** (present or not; not required) | **Multiple chromosomes** in a **nucleus** with nuclear membrane |
| Organelles | **None** | Membrane-bound, specialized |
| Ribosomes | **70S** | **80S** |
| Flagella | Simple (motility) | Complex |
| Cell wall | Complex, with **peptidoglycan** | Only **fungi**, and **without peptidoglycan** |

- **Peptidoglycan is unique to bacteria.**

## 3. Classification of bacteria [Slides p4-6]
- [Slides] By shape and configuration; cell wall structure (**staining**); spore formation; additional structures (capsule, flagella); growth (± oxygen); metabolic/biochemical properties; extracellular or intracellular; genotype. **Microscopic appearance (size, shape, configuration) and the Gram stain are the primary means of distinguishing bacteria.**
- Shapes and arrangements (each cell independent, bound together in nature):

| Shape | Individual | Arrangements |
|---|---|---|
| Spherical | **Coccus** (cocci) | Pairs: **diplococci**; groups of four: **tetrads**; chains (pearl necklace): **streptococci**; clusters (bunch of grapes): **staphylococci** |
| Rod | **Bacillus** (bacilli) | Single; chains: **streptobacilli** |
| Curved rod (comma) | **Vibrio** | |
| Between coccus and rod | **Coccobacillus** | |
| Spiral, short and **rigid** | **Spirillum** | |
| Spiral, longer and **flexible** | **Spirochete** | |

- [Lucía] Examples: diplococcus *Neisseria meningitidis*; *Staphylococcus aureus*; rods *E. coli*; spirochetes *Treponema pallidum*; filamentous *Streptomyces*.

## 4. Gram stain [Slides p6-7]
- The most famous stain in microbiology; developed by **Christian Gram** (Danish). Nearly all bacteria are **Gram-positive** (dark blue/purple) or **Gram-negative** (red/pink); the colour reflects **differences in the cell wall**. Done in about **one hour**: quick structural information.

| Step | Reagent | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Crystal violet** | All cells purple |
| 2 | **Iodine** | Fixes the crystal violet |
| 3 | **Decolorize with alcohol** | **Gram-negative** (thin peptidoglycan) lose the stain, colourless; **Gram-positive** (thick) keep it |
| 4 | **Safranin** (counterstain, red) | Gram-negative turn **pink/red**; Gram-positive stay **purple** |

- **Envelope:**

| | Gram-positive | Gram-negative |
|---|---|---|
| Membranes | **One** (cytoplasmic) | **Two**: cytoplasmic + **outer membrane** |
| Peptidoglycan | **Thick** (lecturer: up to 100 layers; [Slides] ≈50-100 molecules thick) | **Thin** (≈1-2), between the two membranes |
| Permeability | | **More impermeable** to everything (antibiotics, compounds): two barriers |
| Special molecules | **Teichoic and lipoteichoic acids** | **LPS** |

## 5. Peptidoglycan [Slides p7-9]
- **Essential**: bacteria cannot survive without intact peptidoglycan, so it is a great **antibiotic target** (many enzymes in its synthesis; e.g. **penicillin**). [Slides] Beta-lactams (penicillins), glycopeptides.
- A **mesh** ("like a fence") around the cell. Functions: a **sac** keeping the cell contents together; prevents **bursting in hypo-osmotic media** (water enters by osmosis through the semipermeable cytoplasmic membrane); **rigid support** and shape. [Slides] Same.
- **Structure:**
  - Long polymers of alternating **N-acetylglucosamine (NAG)** and **N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM)**, linked **β1-4**.
  - Each **NAM** carries a **tetrapeptide side chain**.
  - Side chains of parallel strands are **cross-linked**.
  - Synthesized with **five** amino acids; the **fifth is cleaved** at the end: mature = tetrapeptide.
- **Unusual amino acids:** **D-amino acids** (nature normally uses L): thought to protect against **proteases/peptidases**, which recognize L-isomers. **Diaminopimelic acid (DAP)**: not one of the 20 amino acids. [Slides] Three amino acids not in proteins: **D-glutamic acid, D-alanine, DAP**.
- **Lysozyme** (innate immunity; tears, saliva, mucus; discovered by **Fleming**, like penicillin) breaks the **β1-4 bond**: bactericidal for most bacteria. [Lucía] Gram-positives are more susceptible to lysozyme (thicker wall).
- **Gram-positive vs Gram-negative peptidoglycan:**

| | Gram-positive | Gram-negative |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-link | **Pentaglycine bridge** (five glycines) | **Direct bond** |
| Between | Amino acid 3 of one chain and 4 of the next | Same (3 and 4) |
| Amino acid 3 | **DAP or L-lysine** (most commonly) or another | **DAP** (almost always) |
| Thickness | Up to 100 layers | 1-2 |

  [Slides] Most Gram-negatives: L-Ala, D-Glu, DAP, D-Ala. **[check]** [Lucía] "meso-diaminopimelic acid (only in Gram negatives)"; slides and lecturer: Gram-positives may also have DAP at position 3.
- Example organisms: ***Escherichia coli*** (Gram-negative; "the most studied organism in the world"); ***Staphylococcus aureus*** (Gram-positive).

## 6. Teichoic and lipoteichoic acids (Gram-positive only) [Slides p9]
- Polymers of **glycerol phosphate** or **ribitol phosphate**, decorated with **amino acids and sugars**.
- **Teichoic acid**: anchored to the **peptidoglycan**. **Lipoteichoic acid**: has a fatty acid, anchored in the **cytoplasmic membrane**.
- **Negatively charged** (phosphate): the main contributors to the negative charge of the bacterial surface (bacterial surfaces are negative, Gram-positive or negative).
- **Roles:** maintain the **structure of the envelope**; **defence** against **antibiotics and host defences**; **adhesion** to host tissues.
- **Example:** about **30%** of people carry ***S. aureus*** in the **nares**; it adheres through teichoic and lipoteichoic acids. [Lucía] They can induce septic shock.

## 7. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) (Gram-negative only) [Slides p10-12]
- The **outer membrane is asymmetric**: inner leaflet **phospholipids**, outer leaflet **entirely LPS**.
- **Essential** for Gram-negatives (only two exceptions, *Acinetobacter* and *Neisseria*: not to be learned).
- **Structure, as a tree:**

| Part | Tree | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Lipid A** | Roots, in the membrane | **Two glucosamines (β1-6)**, usually **two phosphates**, **fatty acids**. **The toxic part** (LPS is very toxic for humans). [Slides] Conserved among related bacteria (similar in all Enterobacteriaceae) |
| **Core** (inner and outer) | Trunk | Sugars: **KDO** (specific to LPS), **heptoses**, **hexoses**, **phosphate** (negative charge); can be modified with **phosphoethanolamine** |
| **O antigen** = O polysaccharide = O side chain | Branches | **Repeating units** of 2 to 8 sugars; **linear or branched**; short or very long, projects far from the cell; **highly variable** between strains: **serotypes** (immunogenic) |

- **O antigen variability:** strains of the same species from different places differ; ***E. coli* has more than 180 O types** (serotype 11, 150...). Each strain has one; it does not switch. This **complicates vaccines**: polysaccharides are the best vaccine candidates, but a vaccine cannot cover 180 variants ("a coat with 180 colours"); a way to **escape immune recognition**. (He worked on vaccines for 22 years.) [Lucía] O antigen up to 200 sugars.
- **Phosphoethanolamine** (positive) added to the core **reduces the negative charge**: resistance to **polymyxin**, a positively charged antibiotic.
- **Amphipathic:** the sugar part (upwards) is **hydrophilic** and excludes hydrophobic molecules; lipid A is **hydrophobic** and excludes hydrophilic molecules. LPS molecules are tightly packed and bind **calcium and magnesium** ions.
- **Functions** [Slides]: **permeability barrier**; stabilizes the outer membrane; protection from **host defences**; **pathogenesis**; **adhesion**; **biofilm formation** (communities of bacteria embedded in polysaccharides; his PhD topic; more later).
- **Smooth vs rough LPS** [Slides p12]:

| | Smooth (S-LPS) | Rough (R-LPS) |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Complete: lipid A, core, **O antigen** | **No O antigen**; complete or truncated core ("deep rough" when part of the core is also lost) |
| Colonies | Smooth, shiny borders | Rough borders |
| Consequence | Wild type, virulent | **More sensitive** to **complement** killing, detergents (**bile salts**), disinfectants; **less virulent** |

- Bacteria survive without the O antigen (but not without the whole LPS). [Slides] Example: colonies of *Yersinia enterocolitica* with S-LPS and R-LPS (see the slide).
- [Lucía] LPS = endotoxin; Gram-negatives also have porins.
