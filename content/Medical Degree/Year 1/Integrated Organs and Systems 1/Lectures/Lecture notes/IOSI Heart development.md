## 1. Origin of the heart
- Mesoderm derivatives (class discussion): **paraxial** mesoderm, somites (vertebrae, skeletal muscle); **intermediate** mesoderm, urogenital system; **lateral** mesoderm, which splits into **somatic** (lines the cavity) and **splanchnic** (visceral, surrounds the viscera and the heart).
- **The heart (myocardium) comes from the splanchnic mesoderm.** [Slides] Splanchnic mesoderm also gives smooth muscle of blood and lymphatic vessels and of the primitive gut derivatives.
- **The cardiovascular system is the first system to function in the embryo.** When does the heart start beating? Students: day 20, 22; lecturer: "more or less". [Lucía] At **day 21**, as soon as the tubes fuse, the heart begins to pump.
- **Cardiogenic field:** myoblasts and **blood islands** from splanchnic mesoderm on **both sides** and in **front** of the embryo (seen through the neural plate).

## 2. Two heart tubes, folding, and the pericardial cavity
- Transverse section: ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm with yolk sac; in the splanchnic layer, **angioblastic cords** on both sides: **two heart tubes** at first.
- Between the two layers of lateral mesoderm, a cavity: the **pericardial cavity**, part of the **intraembryonic coelom**.
- **Craniocaudal folding** (the growing neural tube and brain vesicles fold the head): the heart, initially **in front of the oropharyngeal membrane**, moves to the **chest**.
- **Lateral folding:** the two heart tubes (each with its pericardial cavity) **fuse into one heart tube** surrounded by a single pericardial cavity, about **day 23-24** (lecturer). [Slides] Fusion: figures for day 19 and day 21.
- **Heart and pericardium:** the heart is not inside the cavity; it **pushes into it** (like a fist into a balloon): **visceral (splanchnic) layer** on the heart, **parietal (somatic) layer** outside; a virtual cavity with a little fluid ("two glasses with water between them": hard to separate). The layers reflect into each other at the **great vessels**. These two layers = **serous pericardium**; outside it, the **fibrous pericardium** (thick connective tissue, bounds the middle mediastinum; ends at the great vessels; rests on the diaphragm).
- **Septum transversum**: thick mesoderm in the centre of the embryo; forms the **central tendon of the diaphragm**; the heart is associated with it. (Phrenic nerves on the slide.)

## 3. Parts of the heart tube and looping
- In the single heart tube, blood flows **caudal to cranial**: vessels entering caudally are **veins**, those leaving cranially are **arteries**.

| Part of the heart tube (caudal to cranial) [Slides: SV, A, V, B, TA] | Adult derivative (class summary) |
|---|---|
| **Sinus venosus** (veins enter here) | **Smooth part of the right atrium**; **coronary sinus**; (oblique vein; [Lucía] oblique vein of the left atrium) |
| **Primitive atrium** (single) | **Trabeculated** part of the atria: the **auricles** |
| **Primitive ventricle** | **Trabeculated** parts of the right and left ventricles |
| **Bulbus cordis** (conus) | **Smooth outflow** of the ventricles (infundibulum) |
| **Truncus arteriosus** | **Aorta** and **pulmonary trunk** |

- About **day 21-22** the tube is straight; it **grows** at the atrium and ventricle and **bends on itself**: the **ventricle moves down** (ventral, to the right), the **atrium moves up and back** (dorsal, to the left): **S-shaped heart** by the end of the **4th week**. [Slides] Day 22-24: ventricle ventrally and to the right; atrium dorsally and to the left. [Lucía] C shape at day 24; looping complete by day 28.
- **Growth by incorporating nearby vessels:** veins into the atrium (**smooth** wall), bulbus cordis into the ventricle (smooth outflow). The primitive chambers give the **trabeculated (rough)** parts.
- **External anatomy (anatomical position):** apex = left ventricle; right ventricle in front; the **atrial cavities are posterior (dorsal)**; the **auricles** are appendices of primitive atrium, not real cavities. SVC and IVC enter the right atrium; pulmonary trunk from the right ventricle; aorta from the left; pulmonary veins enter the back of the left atrium.
- **Dorsal mesocardium:** the tube is attached to the dorsal wall by a band of mesoderm that **disappears** (the heart is held by its vessels); the opening becomes the **transverse pericardial sinus**, the space **behind the aorta and pulmonary trunk** (fingers passed behind them; seen on the cadaver).

## 4. Veins and the sinus venosus
- About day 21-22: **paired, symmetrical vessels** (transitory stage) enter the sinus venosus:

| Veins | Carry |
|---|---|
| **Umbilical veins** | **Oxygenated** blood from the placenta (source of oxygen for the embryo) |
| **Vitelline veins** (transcript garbled) | Not stated |
| **Cardinal veins** (superior and inferior) | Body of the embryo (future venae cavae) |

- An **aortic sac** with its arches leaves cranially.
- During weeks 4-8 the **left-sided veins lose importance and mostly disappear**: **left-to-right shift** of venous drainage, so that all drains to the **right atrium**. From the left side only the **coronary sinus** remains. [Slides] Shift primarily due to the left-to-right shunt of blood in the venous system in weeks 4-5.
- 5th week: the sinus venosus opening moves to the right and its wall is **incorporated into the right atrium** (smooth part); the primitive atrium gives the **trabeculated auricle**. The boundary line is the **crista terminalis**.
- Valves from sinus venosus tissue: **valve of the IVC** and **valve of the coronary sinus**; **no valve for the SVC** (blood falls downward with gravity). Student question: why no regurgitation from the SVC? Gravity.
- **Left atrium:** a single **pulmonary vein** opens at first; it branches and is incorporated until **four pulmonary veins** open into it (smooth part); the trabeculated part = auricle.

## 5. Septation: overview and the endocardial cushions
- Weeks **5-7**. Four chambers need: an **atrioventricular septum** (separates atria from ventricles), an **interatrial** septum, an **interventricular** septum, and a septum dividing the **truncus**.
- **Atrioventricular septum:** in the centre of the heart, swellings called **endocardial cushions** appear (anterior and posterior, plus lateral ones; books may say superior and inferior). The **anterior and posterior cushions fuse**: right and left **atrioventricular canals**.
- **Neural crest cells** (from ectoderm, migrating) contribute to the endocardial cushions and form the **atrioventricular valves** (**tricuspid**, **mitral**). [Slides] Endocardial cushions derived from neural crest cells; AV valves, papillary muscles and chordae tendineae (see the slide).

## 6. Atrial septation and fetal circulation
- **Requirement:** before birth the septum must let blood pass **right to left** (oxygenated blood from the umbilical vein reaches the right atrium, but the aorta leaves the left ventricle); **immediately after birth** it must **close**, using material already there.

| Step | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Septum primum** (thin, flexible) grows from the roof towards the endocardial cushions, leaving the **ostium primum** |
| 2 | The **endocardial cushions** grow up and **close the ostium primum** |
| 3 | Before it closes, **apoptosis** opens holes in the upper septum primum: the **ostium secundum** |
| 4 | **Septum secundum** (thicker, to its right) grows down from the roof, covering the ostium secundum but leaving an opening: the **foramen ovale** |
| 5 | Before birth: **higher pressure in the right atrium** pushes the flexible septum primum: blood passes **right to left** |
| 6 | After birth: **higher pressure in the left atrium** presses the septum primum against the septum secundum: **closure of the foramen ovale** |

- Adult right atrium: **fossa ovalis** (thin, from septum primum) with a thicker rim (septum secundum). Student question: both septa are **mesoderm** (myocardium), **not** neural crest.
- **Fetal (prenatal) circulation** ([Slides] three shunts):

| Shunt | Where | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Ductus venosus** | Liver | Oxygenated umbilical blood **bypasses the liver** (not mixed with portal blood) to the IVC |
| **Foramen ovale** | Atria | Right to left: oxygenated blood to the left ventricle and aorta; **first to the head/brain** via the arch branches |
| **Ductus arteriosus** | Pulmonary trunk to aorta | Lungs not functional (in amniotic fluid, high resistance): **about 90%** of right ventricular blood goes to the aorta; joins **after** the arch branches, so the brain gets the best-oxygenated blood |

- Right ventricle receives **mixed** blood (SVC returns deoxygenated blood from the upper body).
- **At birth:** umbilical flow stops, so right atrial pressure falls; the lungs work, so left atrial pressure rises: **foramen ovale closes**. **Ductus arteriosus closes functionally** because of **oxygen** (vasoconstriction) and **fewer prostaglandins** (made by the placenta; they relax the vessel). [Lucía] Bradykinin also constricts the ductus.
- **Remnants** (the four changes):

| Fetal | Adult |
|---|---|
| Umbilical vein | **Ligamentum teres** |
| Ductus venosus | **Ligamentum venosum** |
| Foramen ovale | **Fossa ovalis** |
| Ductus arteriosus | **Ligamentum arteriosum** |

## 7. Atrial septal defects (ASD)
| | **Ostium primum defect** | **Ostium secundum defect** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | **Caudal** part of the septum, near the endocardial cushions | **Cranial** (upper) part |
| Cause | Endocardial cushions do not grow enough to close the ostium primum | **Excess apoptosis** in the septum primum |
| Associated | **AV valve defects** (cushions and neural crest) | No valve defects |

- **Acyanotic:** after birth the pressure is **higher on the left**, so blood flows **left to right**: oxygenated blood goes to the **pulmonary** circulation, not deoxygenated blood to the systemic circulation ("cyanosis" = blue). Not very severe, but pulmonary overload can raise pulmonary pressure/resistance later. [Slides] "Left to right shunt: acyanotic".
- Before birth the pressure was higher on the right (right-to-left flow).

## 8. Ventricular septation and ventricular septal defects (VSD)
- Not two septa side by side (as in the atria) but parts that must **meet end to end**:
  - **Muscular** part: grows up from the **floor** of the ventricle (trabeculated).
  - **Membranous** part: from the **endocardial cushions** and the **bulbus cordis** (conus) (smooth outflow).
- Three elements must align in the midline (muscular septum, endocardial cushions, truncal ridges): easily misaligned.
- **VSD: very common** (more than ASD); **acyanotic** (higher pressure on the left: left-to-right shunt).

## 9. Truncus arteriosus septation and tetralogy of Fallot
- Two swellings (**conotruncal ridges**): the right grows distally **to the left**, the left distally **to the right**: a **spiral septum** dividing the truncus into **aorta** and **pulmonary trunk**. Because of the spiral, the two vessels leave the heart twisted around each other (frontal view; transcript partly garbled, see the slide). [Slides] Neural crest cells contribute to the endocardial cushions in **both the conus cordis and the truncus arteriosus**.
- **Semilunar valves** (aortic, pulmonary) also form here with neural crest contribution. Cusp positions (pulmonary: anterior, right, left; aortic: posterior, right, left; transcript garbled **[verify]**): "not very important now".
- **Tetralogy of Fallot:** the truncal septum is **shifted to the right** (not in the midline): **pulmonary stenosis**, the one defect that causes the other three:

| Element | Mechanism (lecturer) |
|---|---|
| **Pulmonary stenosis** | Displaced septum |
| **Ventricular septal defect** | The septum does not close properly |
| **Overriding aorta** | Aorta over both ventricles |
| **Right ventricular hypertrophy** | The ventricle pushes against the narrowed vessel |

- Cyanotic or acyanotic? The transcript answer is garbled ("lower pressure on the right side") **[verify]**. [Lucía] Tetralogy of Fallot is **cyanotic** (the overriding aorta receives deoxygenated blood).
- [Slides only] Other anomalies: persistent truncus arteriosus, transposition of the great arteries, aortic stenosis (see the slides).
