## 1. Pericardium
- **Fibrous pericardium**: outer, thick connective tissue sac.
- **Serous pericardium**, two layers:
  - **Parietal** layer: lines the inside of the fibrous pericardium.
  - **Visceral** layer: attached to the heart = **epicardium** (same structure; "you've already seen the histology").
  - Between them the **pericardial cavity**.
- [Slides] Fibrous pericardium shown in situ; "Cardiac orientation: cardiac walls".

## 2. Innervation of the pericardium and of the heart
| Layer | Nerve | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous** pericardium and **parietal serous** pericardium | **Phrenic nerves** (on both sides of the middle mediastinum, on their way to the diaphragm) | Sensory (afferent) fibres from **C3-C5**: pain from **pericarditis** is felt at that level ("at this point up here, C3-C5") |
| **Visceral** pericardium (epicardium) and heart | **Autonomic nervous system** | Parasympathetic (vagus) + sympathetic (sympathetic trunk), through the **cardiac plexus** inside the pericardial sac |

- **Parasympathetic:** **vagus nerves**; preganglionic neurons in the **brainstem** (**dorsal nucleus of the vagus nerve**, named in the 24 Nov session, section 10b); fibres come down as **preganglionic** fibres; the second neurons are in ganglia **at the heart** (in the plexus).
- **Sympathetic:** first neurons in the **lateral horn** of spinal segments **T1-T5** (students said T1; lecturer: "T5, more or less"); synapse in the **sympathetic trunk** at the same levels; **postganglionic** fibres go to the heart as **cardiac (sympathetic) nerves**. [Slides] Cardiac innervation (T1-T5).
- So the cardiac plexus near the great vessels contains **preganglionic** vagal fibres and **postganglionic** sympathetic fibres.
- **Student question, cardiac nerves vs splanchnic nerves:** the thoracic **splanchnic** nerves are **T5-L1**; they cross the diaphragm and synapse in the **preaortic ganglia** to supply the abdominal viscera (digestive system). The sympathetic fibres to the heart are called **cardiac nerves**, not splanchnic nerves.
- **Superficial and deep cardiac plexuses**, afferents and **referred pain from the heart**: lectured 24 Nov, section 10b-c.

## 3. External configuration: the heart in anatomical position
- **Apex** = **left ventricle**; points **down, to the left and anterior**.
- **Anterior (sternocostal) surface** = mainly the **right ventricle**. The left ventricle forms the apex and continues **posteriorly onto the diaphragmatic surface**.
- **Interventricular sulcus** (anterior and posterior): separates right and left ventricles (the interventricular septum).
- **Atrioventricular (coronary) sulcus**: separates atria from ventricles; corresponds to the **endocardial cushions** of the embryo.
- **Auricles** (right and left): the only parts of the atria seen from the front; **appendages, not real cavities**, from the primitive atrium. **Both atria are posterior.**
- **Great vessels from the front:** **pulmonary trunk anterior and on the left** (although it leaves the right ventricle); **aorta posterior and on the right** (although it leaves the left ventricle): the result of the **spiral septum** (see [[IOSI Heart development]]). **SVC** enters the right atrium from above.
- **Posterior view:** left ventricle below (diaphragmatic surface), right ventricle, posterior interventricular sulcus; **right and left atria**; in the **posterior atrioventricular sulcus**, the **coronary sinus** (the vein draining the heart itself, from the sinus venosus), opening into the right atrium next to the IVC. Small **cardiac veins** all drain into the coronary sinus (section 10).
- **Left atrium:** **four pulmonary veins** (two left, two right). As in the right atrium, the **walls of the veins are incorporated** into the atrium: the part from the vein walls = the **real cavity**; the anterior **auricle = primitive atrium**.

## 4. Right atrium
- Forms the **right border** of the heart. Six walls ("faces"): superior, inferior, septal (medial), lateral, anterior, posterior. [Slides] Netter figure of the walls.

| Wall | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Superior** | Opening of the **SVC** (upper posterior part); start of the **crista terminalis** on the roof, just in front of the SVC opening [Slides] |
| **Septal (medial)** = interatrial septum | **Fossa ovalis** (thin; from the **septum primum**, the old valve of the foramen ovale, closed against the septum secundum after birth by the higher left atrial pressure); its thicker rim = **limbus of the fossa ovalis** (where the **septum secundum** ends). A finger in the fossa ovalis can almost be seen through it |
| **Inferior** | Opening of the **IVC** with the **valve of the IVC**; opening of the **coronary sinus** with the **valve of the coronary sinus** (both small folds from the valve of the sinus venosus; prevent backflow) [Slides] |
| **Lateral** | **Crista terminalis** and the **pectinate muscles** fanning out from it "like the teeth of a comb" [Slides]: the rough, trabeculated inner surface of the auricle |
| **Anterior** | **Right atrioventricular orifice** with the **tricuspid valve**; faces forward and medially [Slides]; right auricle |
| **Posterior** | Listed among the six walls; not described in class or on the slides |

- **Crista terminalis** = the boundary between the **rough** part (primitive atrium: auricle, pectinate muscles) and the **smooth** part (from the veins/sinus venosus).
- **No valve on the SVC:** blood comes down with gravity.
- The **IVC** passes through the diaphragm at **T8** and **enters the right atrium directly** (no thoracic course in the mediastinum).
- [Slides] Autoevaluation labels: 1 SVC, 2 interatrial septum, 3 fossa ovalis, 4 IVC, 5 crista terminalis, 6 pectinate muscles, 7 valve of IVC, 8 valve of coronary sinus.

## 5. Right ventricle
- Forms most of the **anterior surface** and part of the **diaphragmatic** surface [Slides]. Three walls: **anterior, posterior, septal**.
- Two portions (from the two embryonic parts of the septum):
  - **Inflow**: muscular, rough, **trabeculae carneae**.
  - **Outflow**: **conus arteriosus (infundibulum)**, **smooth**, from the **bulbus cordis**; leads to the pulmonary trunk.
- **Tricuspid valve**: three cusps, **anterior, posterior, septal** (one per ventricular wall). Each cusp is attached by **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles** (anterior, posterior, septal), whose bases sit on the corresponding walls.
- **How it works ("inverted umbrella"):** papillary muscle = handle, chordae = ribs, cusps = canopy. Like a door that opens only one way: blood passes when atrial pressure is higher. In **systole** the walls and papillary muscles contract, the chordae go taut and the valve closes, **preventing prolapse** of the cusps and backflow into the atrium; blood must leave through the outflow tract to the pulmonary trunk.
- **Septomarginal trabecula (moderator band)** [Slides]: part of the conduction system, a shortcut from the right branch of the bundle of His to the anterior papillary muscle (24 Nov, section 10a).
- **Pulmonary valve**: three **semilunar** cusps, **anterior, right, left**. Each cusp forms a small pocket (**pulmonary sinus**). When blood flows back it **fills the pockets**, pushing the cusps to the centre: the valve closes. Each cusp has a **nodule** in the middle (when the valve closes the nodules meet in the centre, closing it completely) and **lunules** [Slides: nodule, lunule labels]. Needed because blood in the pulmonary trunk flows upwards.

## 6. Left atrium
- Seen from the front only as the **left auricle**; the atrium itself is on the **posterior** wall and forms the **base** of the heart.
- **Esophagus directly posterior to the left atrium.** A dilated left atrium (e.g. **mitral valve disease**) can compress it: **dysphagia** (difficulty swallowing) may be a sign of left atrial enlargement.
- Walls [Slides]: anterior (mitral valve), lateral (left auricle), medial (**valve of the foramen ovale**: the other side of the fossa ovalis), posterior (pulmonary veins; esophagus), superior, inferior.
- **Mitral (bicuspid) valve**: **two cusps (anterior, posterior)**, **two papillary muscles (anterior, posterior)**, thicker; chordae tendineae; same mechanism as the tricuspid. Surrounded by its fibrous ring.

## 7. Left ventricle
- **Wall about three times thicker than the right**: it pumps into the **systemic circulation** at much higher pressure.
- Anterior and posterior papillary muscles, chordae tendineae, trabeculae carneae; an outflow part to the aorta that she called **membranous**, "like the same in the right".
- **Aortic valve**: three semilunar cusps, **posterior, right, left** (the opposite of the pulmonary). Memory aid: **the aorta is posterior, so its odd cusp is posterior; the pulmonary trunk is anterior, so its odd cusp is anterior.** Same nodules and lunules.
- **Coronary arteries**: two openings beside the aortic valve, one **right** and one **left**: the **first branches of the aorta** (systemic circulation), supplying the heart itself.

## 8. Cardiac skeleton
- **Fibrous rings** around the four valves, in one plane. The lecturer called them "rings of cartilage... connective tissue" **[check]**: the histology slides call them fibrous rings, see [[IOSI Histology of the cardiovascular system I]] section 5.
- Functions:
  1. **Separates the atrial from the ventricular myocardium**, so the atria contract first, then the ventricles.
  2. **Keeps the orifices open and in place** while the heart beats.
  3. **Attachment** for the cusps of the valves and for the **myocardial fibres**.
- **Drawing to know** (transverse section, atria removed): posterior **tricuspid** (right) and **mitral** (left); anterior to them the **aortic** valve (right, posterior of the two arteries) and **pulmonary** valve (left, most anterior).

| Phase | AV valves (tricuspid, mitral) | Semilunar valves (pulmonary, aortic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Diastole** (ventricles fill) | **Open** | **Closed** |
| **Systole** (ventricles eject) | **Closed** | **Open** |

## 9. Layers of the heart and the myocardium
- **Epicardium** (visceral layer of the serous pericardium), **myocardium** (thick muscle), **endocardium** (thin inner layer) [Slides].
- Myocardium of the ventricles: fibres **insert on the fibrous rings** (caudal side). Two kinds (partly garbled):
  - **"B" fibres**: start on one ring, **wrap around the (other) ventricle and return** to the same ring **[verify]** (garbled);
  - **"A" fibres**: run from **one ring to the other** ring.
  - Result: the ventricular myocardium is **much thicker than the atrial** and arranged in **three layers** (superficial, middle, deep; [Slides] Putz-Pabst figure); she linked the **A fibres** to the **interventricular septum** **[verify]** (garbled; check with the Putz-Pabst and Basmajian figures on the slides). ## 10. Conduction system, cardiac plexuses and afferents, coronary arteries and cardiac veins (24 Nov)
From [[Universidad de Navarra/Medical Degree/Year 1/Transcripts/Integrated Organs And Systems 1/cardiac anatomy 3.txt|IOSI/cardiac anatomy 3]] (the extra session).

### 10a. Cardiac conduction system [Slides p35, Netter]
- The heart beats **spontaneously**: specialized muscle fibres produce and coordinate the contraction.

| Element | Where | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Sinoatrial (SA) node** ("the pacemaker of the heart") | Right atrium, at the **superior end of the crista terminalis**, at the opening of the **superior vena cava** (the crista separates the rough wall from the smooth wall of the atrium, section 4) | **Initiates** and coordinates the contraction; the wave passes through the walls of both atria |
| 2. **Atrioventricular (AV) node** | **Atrioventricular septum**, close to where the **coronary sinus** enters the right atrium | The atrial wave converges here |
| 3. **Bundle of His** (atrioventricular bundle) | From the AV node through the **interventricular septum** | Splits into a **right branch** (right ventricle) and a **left branch** (left ventricle) |
| 4. **Purkinje fibres** | An **endocardial plexus** of fibres in each ventricle, through the trabeculae | Spread the wave through the ventricles |

- **Shortcut to the anterior papillary muscle:** from the **right branch**, the **septomarginal trabecula** (= septomarginal bundle = **moderator band**) runs from the septum to the **anterior papillary muscle** of the right ventricle. So the papillary muscle contracts **first**, the tricuspid valve closes, and then the wave passes to the rest of the plexus. It is part of the conduction system.
- Histology of the nodes and Purkinje fibres: [[IOSI Histology of the cardiovascular system I]] section 5.

### 10b. Autonomic control of the heart: efferent pathways [Slides p36-39]
- The rhythm is spontaneous but can be **modulated** by the nervous system: heart rate, force of contraction, and the **coronary arteries** (dilation or constriction).

| | Parasympathetic | Sympathetic |
|---|---|---|
| 1st (preganglionic) neuron | **Dorsal nucleus of the vagus nerve** (brainstem) | **Lateral horn of T1-T5** [Slides p36: T1-T5] (students' answer garbled, "G1, G4"; she said "perfect" **[verify]**) |
| Route | Preganglionic fibres descend in the **vagus nerves** | Ventral root, spinal nerve, **white ramus**, **sympathetic trunk ganglion**; postganglionic fibres to the heart |
| 2nd (postganglionic) neuron | **Close to the heart**: small ganglia in the cardiac plexus / heart wall | In the sympathetic trunk: some **thoracic ganglia at the same levels (T1-T5)**, some **cervical ganglia** higher up |
| Nerves to the heart | Vagal fibres to the cardiac plexus | **Cervical and thoracic (sympathetic) cardiac nerves**: named after the ganglion where they synapse; all **postganglionic** |
| Effect | **Decreases** the heart rate (and contraction, garbled **[verify]**); neurotransmitter not legible in the transcript **[verify]** | **Increases** heart rate and probably force (transcript "the pores" **[verify]**); **dilation of the coronary arteries** (neurotransmitter garbled, "the normal learning" **[verify]**) |

- **Two cardiac plexuses** [Slides p37-39], both **mixed** (sympathetic + parasympathetic) and **efferent** (motor) fibres:
  - **Superficial cardiac plexus**: **anterior** to the **arch of the aorta**.
  - **Deep cardiac plexus**: **posterior** to the arch of the aorta.
  - Class question: in these plexuses the vagal (parasympathetic) fibres are **preganglionic** ("look at the picture"); the sympathetic fibres are postganglionic (synapse already in the sympathetic chain).

### 10c. Afferent (sensory) fibres from the heart
| Sensation | Travels with | Pathway |
|---|---|---|
| **Distension / pressure** (e.g. **baroreceptors** in the arch of the aorta) | **Parasympathetic** (vagus) | Sensory neuron in a **ganglion of the vagus outside the CNS**, near the medulla (transcript "interventoran gumbion" **[verify]** the name), then the **nucleus of the solitary tract** (sensory nucleus), which connects with the motor nucleus: response such as **bradycardia** and coronary vasodilation. "Not important now": you will study it in IOS II |
| **Pain** (e.g. **ischaemia, myocardial infarction** of the ventricle) | **Sympathetic** ("in most cases, pain" travels with the sympathetic nerves) | Chemical agents stimulate receptors; fibres run back with the sympathetic cardiac nerves, through the **white ramus**, to the **dorsal root ganglion** of the corresponding spinal nerve (sensory neuron), into the **dorsal horn**, synapse, cross to the other side and ascend to the brain |

- Student question: in both pathways the sensory neuron connects to the motor side directly, "no interneurons" (her answer).
- **Referred pain from the heart: "you have to know this."** Pain from an infarct or ischaemia of the ventricle is felt not only in the **chest** but also in the **left arm**. Reason: **convergence** of visceral afferents from the heart with **somatic afferents** from the left arm entering the **same spinal cord segments** (same spinal nerves, same dermatomes): mainly **T1-T2** (transcript "P1, P2"). Clinical: a man with risk factors and pain in the chest and arm: suspect an infarction. [Slides only, figure] "Referred pain from the heart".

### 10d. Coronary arteries [Slides p41-43]
- Best studied in the **dissection room**; the group leaders are dissecting them now in last year's hearts.

| Artery | Course | Branches |
|---|---|---|
| **Right coronary artery (RCA)** | From the aorta to the right, in the **right atrioventricular sulcus**, then round to the **posterior** surface | **Sinoatrial nodal branch** (usually from the RCA; to the SA node), **right marginal artery** (at the right border), **posterior interventricular artery** (= posterior descending; its main branch) |
| **Left coronary artery (LCA)** | Short **common trunk** passing **between the pulmonary trunk and the left auricle** | **Anterior interventricular artery** (down the anterior interventricular sulcus) and **circumflex branch** (round the left side to the posterior wall), which gives the **left marginal** artery |

- **Dominance** (which artery gives the posterior interventricular artery):

| Pattern | Lecturer |
|---|---|
| **Right dominance**: posterior interventricular from the **RCA** | The normal pattern: "65 to 85%" of people; later "usually from the right coronary, **80%**" |
| **Left dominance**: posterior interventricular from the **circumflex** | "About 10% or less"; the LCA then supplies the anterior left part and the posterior part |
| **Co-dominance** | Two posterior interventricular branches, one from each side |

- "**The pattern that you have to learn is this one**, the most important and most common, but you have to know that **variations are very common**" (they will look for anomalies in dissection).

| Posterior (cardiac) vein | Posterior wall (mentioned only in passing **[verify]**; not on the slide text) | Coronary sinus |
