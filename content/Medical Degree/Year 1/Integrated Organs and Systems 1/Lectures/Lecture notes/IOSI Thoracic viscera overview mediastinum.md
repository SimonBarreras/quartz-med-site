## 1. Mediastinum: boundaries and divisions
- The space in the centre of the thorax, **between the two pleural cavities** (both lungs are surrounded by the pleural cavities). [Slides p2]

| Boundary | Structure |
|---|---|
| Anterior | **Sternum** ([Lucía] body of the sternum) |
| Posterior | **Thoracic vertebrae** |
| Lateral | **Pleural cavities** |
| Superior | **Superior thoracic aperture** |
| Inferior | **Diaphragm** |

- [Slides p2-3] Same five boundaries. On the second diagram (Gray's) she says "anteriorly we have the diaphragm" **[verify]**: a slip; the slides and her first list give the diaphragm as the inferior boundary.
- **Imaginary line** at the level of the **sternal angle** anteriorly and **between T4 and T5** posteriorly divides the mediastinum into:
  - **Superior mediastinum.**
  - **Inferior mediastinum**, subdivided by the heart in the pericardium into **anterior** (in front of the heart), **middle** (the heart within the pericardium) and **posterior** (behind the heart). [Slides p4-5] [Gray's] The plane passes through the sternal angle and the intervertebral disc between TIV and TV; the pericardium and heart form the middle mediastinum.

## 2. Superior mediastinum: thymus
- First element: a **gland**, the **thymus**. Lies in the superior mediastinum, reaches **the neck** above and passes down into the **inferior** mediastinum. [Slides p6] Thymus in front of the great vessels, above the pericardial sac; the TIV/V vertebral level line is drawn across it.
- System: **immune** (a student said endocrine; she corrected: immune). Most important **in children**, where it produces the lymphocytes (T cells). In the adult, the transcript is garbled ("we are not going to see a time of training, we are going to see like partitions") **[verify]**. [Lucía] Much bigger in children; after puberty it atrophies and is replaced by fat; in adults only fatty remnants.
- **Arterial supply:** branches of the **internal thoracic arteries**. [Slides p6] Right and left internal thoracic arteries labelled.
- Student question: if the thymus atrophies, do we produce fewer T cells? No: other elements of the immune system take over; this is studied in FMC II (From the molecule to the cell II).

## 3. Superior mediastinum: great vessels
### Veins
- Main vein draining the upper body: **superior vena cava (SVC)**, on the **right** side (the right heart carries the deoxygenated blood).
- **Brachiocephalic veins**, right and left, each formed by the **internal jugular** and **subclavian** veins.
- The **left brachiocephalic vein crosses the midline** to the right; the two brachiocephalic veins join to form the **SVC**, which enters the **right atrium**. [Slides p8] Right and left brachiocephalic veins, SVC; SVC at the TIV/TV level.
- Two general patterns she points out:
  - **Veins lie anterior to the arteries** ("the veins protect the arteries"): the left brachiocephalic vein passes in front of the main branches of the arch.
  - Veins of the **left side cross the midline** to the right in many places (example: the left brachiocephalic vein).
- **Child vs adult:** in the adult the left brachiocephalic vein is covered by the **manubrium**; in the child it **rises above the superior border of the manubrium**, so it is **less protected**. [Slides p7] Same.

### Arteries
- **Ascending aorta** from the **left ventricle** (oxygenated blood), then the **arch of the aorta**, then the **descending aorta**, which passes to the posterior mediastinum.
- **Branches of the arch (three, "you have to know them"):**

| Order | Branch | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brachiocephalic trunk** (artery) | Right common carotid + right subclavian |
| 2 | **Left common carotid** | (direct from the arch) |
| 3 | **Left subclavian** | (direct from the arch) |

- [Slides p7] "The arch of the aorta is at the superior mediastinum, 3 important branches ... They are crossed anteriorly by the left brachiocephalic vein."

### Trachea and esophagus
- **Trachea** in the midline, continuing the larynx; she points to the tracheal cartilages at the **jugular notch**. **Esophagus posterior to the trachea.** [Slides p7] Trachea, right and left main bronchi labelled. [Lucía] Trachea gives the main bronchi at T4-T5.

## 4. Superior mediastinum: vagus nerves and recurrent laryngeal nerves
- **Vagus nerves**, right and left, descend lateral to the trachea and esophagus. Branch: the **recurrent laryngeal nerve**, which supplies the **larynx**.
- **Left recurrent laryngeal nerve:** lower, at the level of the **arch of the aorta**, at the **ligamentum arteriosum**. [Slides p8-9] Left vagus, left recurrent laryngeal nerve, arch of the aorta, ligamentum arteriosum.
- **Right recurrent laryngeal nerve:** higher, around the **carotid and subclavian** arteries ("at the level of the bifurcation between the carotid and the subclavian"). [Lucía] At the level of the bifurcation of the brachiocephalic artery. [Gray's] It loops around the right subclavian artery.
- **Ligamentum arteriosum** (= former **ductus arteriosus**): before birth the lungs are not functional, so a vessel connects the **pulmonary trunk** with the **aorta** to bypass them. After birth it stops carrying blood and becomes the ligamentum arteriosum. [Slides p8] "Ligamentum arteriosum, *ductus arteriosus".
- **Origin of the vagus:** brainstem, **medulla oblongata** (of midbrain, pons, medulla). Preganglionic **parasympathetic** neurons that supply the viscera of the **thorax and abdomen**; the vagus also carries **somatic** fibres for the larynx. (Later in the lecture a student names the dorsal nucleus of the vagus in the medulla.)
- **Why the two recurrent nerves differ (embryology):** [Slides p9, Moore-Persaud]
  - The embryo has **pharyngeal arches** in the neck, each with its artery (**aortic arches**, from the aortic sac) and its nerve.
  - Initially the recurrent laryngeal nerve hooks around the **6th aortic arch** on both sides.
  - **Right side:** the 6th (distal part) and 5th arches **disappear**, so the nerve **moves up** and hooks around the vessel that forms the **subclavian** (at the carotid-subclavian level).
  - **Left side:** the 6th arch gives rise to the **ductus arteriosus**, so the nerve **stays** at that point: **lower** than on the right.
  - She will show a video.

## 6. Anterior mediastinum
- Small space **anterior to the pericardium**; "not many things":
  - **Internal thoracic vessels** (artery and vein).
  - From them, the **anterior intercostal vessels**: below each rib a small **vein, artery and nerve** (V-A-N).
  - **Ligaments from the sternum to the pericardium** ([Lucía] sternopericardial ligaments).
- [Slides p13, Netter] Phrenic nerves and pericardiophrenic vessels; internal thoracic vessels; anterior intercostal vein/artery/nerve; **mediastinal branches of the internal thoracic vessels**. [Slides p14] Fixed specimen (formalin): internal thoracic vessels and the intercostal spaces; to be seen on the cadavers.
- [Lucía] The inferior part of the thymus can be seen here in children.

## 7. Middle mediastinum and phrenic nerves
- Contains the **heart within the fibrous pericardium** (a sac covering the heart); heart and pericardium next session. [Slides p15-17] Fixed specimens; see the slides.
- **Specimen exercise (slide 17):** identify the left and right brachiocephalic veins, the internal jugular and subclavian veins, the **brachiocephalic trunk**, the right common carotid. **Arteries have thicker, muscular walls; veins are thinner.**
- **Phrenic nerves:** on **both sides of the heart**, **lateral, on the fibrous pericardium** (easy to find): the **left phrenic** on the left border of the heart, the **right phrenic** on the right; they supply the **diaphragm**. (She first pointed to two nerves as phrenic, then corrected: those were the vagus nerves.) [Slides p13] Phrenic nerves with the pericardiophrenic vessels.
- **Origin: C3 to C5** (a student said C3-C6; she: "I would say C3-C5"). [Lucía] C3-C4-C5. [Gray's] Anterior rami of C3, C4 and C5, mainly C4.
- **Somatic**, not autonomic, because the **diaphragm is a skeletal muscle**.
  - **Motor (efferent):** neurons in the **ventral horn** of C3-C5.
  - **Sensory (afferent):** the fibres enter through the dorsal root; the neuron body is outside, in the **dorsal root ganglion**.
  - The phrenic nerves give **motor and sensory** innervation to the diaphragm. [Lucía] They also give sensory branches to the mediastinal pleura and the pericardium.
- **Is diaphragm contraction voluntary?** You can contract it voluntarily, but breathing is **automatic**: brainstem nuclei control it automatically, not the autonomic nervous system (studied later in the nervous system). The neurons that finally contract the diaphragm are the ventral horn neurons of the phrenic nerve.

## 8. Posterior mediastinum: esophagus
- **Begins at C6**, at the **cricoid cartilage** (thyroid cartilage above it), as the continuation of the **pharynx** (pharynx and larynx: neck, later course). [Slides p19] Pharynx, cricoid cartilage (C6).
- Muscular tube with **longitudinal** and **circular** muscle layers (digestive system later, "with Dr. Andrea probably" **[verify]** the name).
- **Trachea anterior** to it. The tracheal cartilages are **incomplete posteriorly**: the posterior wall is **membranous**, because the esophagus behind it changes shape as food passes.
- Course: superior mediastinum to the posterior part of the inferior mediastinum. **At first to the right of the thoracic aorta** (aorta on the left); lower down it **crosses anterior to the aorta** to the left to pass through the diaphragm to the **stomach** (on the left).
- **Relationships ("important to know"):** the **left main bronchus** passes **anterior** to the esophagus, so an anomaly of one can affect the other; an **aortic aneurysm** can **compress the esophagus**. [Slides p20] Relationships: **thoracic duct** (posteriorly); **mediastinal pleura** on both sides; **thoracic aorta** (on the left); **right pulmonary artery and left main bronchus**; **left atrium** (anteriorly). Esophageal hiatus (TX).

### The four constrictions (exam flag)

| Level | Constriction |
|---|---|
| **C6** | Beginning: junction with the pharynx, at the cricoid cartilage |
| **T4** | Where it is crossed by the **arch of the aorta** (contact with its anterior part) |
| **T5** | Where it is compressed by the **left main bronchus** |
| **T10** | **Esophageal hiatus** of the diaphragm (passage into the abdomen) |

- [Slides p21] Same four, with levels C6, T4, T5, T10. [Lucía] Same.
- **Clinical:** a swallowed object most probably **lodges** at a constriction; a **corrosive fluid** passes more slowly there and damages the mucosa more; clinicians passing **instruments** must know where the narrowings are. [Lucía] A child's swallowed object is most likely found at these narrowings.

### Nerves of the esophagus
- **Vagus nerves:** the **left vagus passes anterior** and the **right vagus posterior** to the esophagus; their fibres go through the **esophageal hiatus** to the abdominal viscera (the **anterior** and **posterior vagal trunks**). [Slides p22] Right and left vagus, **esophageal plexus**, **anterior vagal trunk**; sympathetic **3rd to 6th thoracic ganglia** also send fibres to the plexus. [Lucía] Same anterior/posterior sides.
- **How far does the vagus reach?** Up to **two thirds of the transverse colon** (student answer, accepted). **[check]** [Lucía] "up till the distal third of the TC"; the wording is ambiguous; Gray's ch. 4 did not settle it in the passages checked.
- The vagal fibres seen on the esophagus are **preganglionic**: the parasympathetic ganglia are small and close to or in the wall of the organs, so the **postganglionic fibres are very short**.
- **Sympathetic: thoracic splanchnic nerves** (named by the students):
  - Preganglionic neurons in the **lateral horn** of the spinal cord.
  - The fibres leave through the **white ramus communicans**, **pass through the paravertebral ganglion without synapsing**, cross the diaphragm and **synapse in the preaortic (prevertebral) ganglia** on the aorta; postganglionic fibres follow the arteries to the viscera. [Slides p30] "Preganglionic sympathetic axons arise from T5-L2,3 cord segments, and PASS THROUGH paravertebral ganglia ... becomes a root of a splanchnic nerve, and will synapse in a prevertebral ganglion"; postganglionic axons innervate viscera below the diaphragm.
  - Levels (garbled): T1-T4 fibres go to the **heart** (next week); the splanchnic nerves from **T5** down ("from T5 to ...") **[verify]** the lower level. Fibres from about T3/T4 to T5 "sometimes go directly to the heart and the pulmonary plexus" **[verify]**. [Gray's] Greater splanchnic nerve: from the 5th to 9th or 10th thoracic ganglia; lesser: 9th-10th or 10th-11th; they pass through the crura of the diaphragm.
  - The esophageal plexus receives both the vagus and the sympathetic fibres.

### Vessels of the esophagus
- Arteries: **branches of the descending (thoracic) aorta**. [Slides p23] Arterial supply: esophageal branch of the **inferior thyroid artery**, right bronchial artery, superior and inferior left bronchial arteries, **aortic esophageal arteries**, **ascending branches of the left gastric artery**.
- Veins: **esophageal veins drain into the azygos system**, which drains into the caval system. [Slides p23] Venous drainage (Netter): azygos, hemiazygos, accessory hemiazygos, intercostal veins, SVC.

## 9. Posterior mediastinum: azygos system
- A system **parallel to the caval system**: the **azygos vein** on the right; the **hemiazygos** and **accessory hemiazygos** veins on the left. They drain the **lateral (and posterior) body walls** through the **intercostal veins**, and the **esophagus**.
- **Origin:** below the diaphragm, the **ascending lumbar veins** (lower body walls); on crossing the diaphragm, on the right side they form the **azygos vein**.
- **Right side:** the azygos receives the **subcostal vein** (12) and the posterior **intercostal veins** 11, 10, 9 ... upwards; it arches over to end in the **SVC** (arch of the azygos). [Slides p25, Grant's] Azygos v., arch of azygos, SVC.
- **Left side:** the **hemiazygos** (lower intercostal veins) and the **accessory hemiazygos** (8th to 5th) either drain separately or **join**, then **cross the midline** to the azygos ("veins on the left side have to pass to the right side"). The transcript lists for the hemiazygos "12th, 11th, 8th, 9th" **[verify]**. [Lucía] Hemiazygos 9-12, accessory hemiazygos 5-8, both into the azygos. [Slides p25] Hemiazygos v. lower, accessory hemiazygos v. above; both cross to the azygos.
- The **azygos is thicker** because it receives blood from both sides.
- **Upper intercostal spaces:**

| Vein | Drains | Into |
|---|---|---|
| **Right superior intercostal vein** | Three spaces together (2nd-4th) | **Azygos vein** |
| **Left superior intercostal vein** | Upper left spaces | **Left brachiocephalic vein** (closer) |
| **Supreme intercostal vein** | **1st** intercostal space | Brachiocephalic vein of its side; the **left** one is small, seen in some cadavers |

  [Slides p25] Right and left supreme intercostal veins; right and left superior intercostal veins. [Lucía] Right supreme into the right brachiocephalic; left 2-4 form the left superior intercostal vein into the left brachiocephalic, with the left supreme. [Gray's] Right superior intercostal vein = 2nd, 3rd and 4th intercostal veins, into the azygos; left superior intercostal vein into the left brachiocephalic vein.
- **Clinical:** there are **anastomoses between the azygos and the caval systems**; if part of the caval system is **blocked**, blood can return to the heart **through the azygos system**.

## 10. Posterior mediastinum: thoracic aorta
- From the **arch**, the **descending aorta** gives the **posterior intercostal arteries** ("that you are going to dissect in the cadaver"). It passes the diaphragm at the **aortic hiatus, T12**. [Slides p24] Posterior intercostal arteries; aortic hiatus TXII; also bronchial, mediastinal, esophageal, pericardial branches, superior phrenic and subcostal arteries.

## 11. Posterior mediastinum: thoracic duct
- The way for **lymph to reach the venous system**.
- At **T12**, a small sac full of lymph: the **cisterna chyli**. [Slides p27] Cisterna chyli. [Lucía] Begins at T12. [Gray's] Begins in the abdomen as a confluence of lymphatic trunks, sometimes forming a saccular dilatation, the cisterna chyli.
- The **thoracic duct** ascends in the posterior mediastinum, **anterior to the thoracic vertebrae**, and passes to the **left** to drain at the **junction of the left internal jugular and subclavian veins**. [Lucía] Passes to the left at T5-T6. [Gray's] At TV it moves to the left of the midline and enters the superior mediastinum; ends at the junction of the left subclavian and left internal jugular veins.
- **Main lymphatic duct of the body.** Drains the **lower body** and, above, the **left side** (head, arm, thorax).
- **Right lymphatic duct:** smaller; drains the **right side of the upper body** (head, arm, thorax). [Slides p27] Drainage areas of the right lymphatic duct and the thoracic duct.
- Along the system there are lymph nodes.
