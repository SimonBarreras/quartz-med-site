## 1. Components of the cardiovascular system [Slides]
1. **Heart**: the pump that sends blood to all tissues and organs.
2. **Blood vascular system**: connects the heart and the tissues.
3. **Lymphatic vascular system** (end of the block): returns excess tissue fluid to the blood.

## 2. The heart: chambers, valves and the three layers
- Four chambers: two **ventricles** (pump) and two **atria** (receive); interventricular and interatrial septa. Similar histological composition in all four. [Slides] Right ventricle: blood to the lungs; left ventricle: to the rest of the body; right atrium receives from the body; left atrium from the pulmonary veins.
- Valves: **atrioventricular** (tricuspid right, mitral left) and **semilunar** (pulmonary and aortic) at the start of the arteries leaving the heart.
- **Three layers** (inside to outside):

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **Endocardium** (internal) | **Endothelium** (simple squamous) + subendothelial connective tissue; **subendocardial layer**: connective tissue with small vessels, nerves and **Purkinje fibers** (mostly in the ventricles). [Slides] Thin loose CT with elastic and collagen fibres and some smooth muscle cells |
| **Myocardium** (middle, **thickest**) | Cardiac muscle |
| **Epicardium** (external) = **visceral pericardium** | Fibroelastic CT with **adipocytes**, then **mesothelium** facing the pericardial cavity |

- **Endothelium** = the epithelium of the cardiovascular system: the **only** histological component present in **all** parts of the cardiovascular system.
- **Wall thickness:** **ventricles thicker than atria** (they pump), mainly the myocardium. **Left ventricle thicker than the right**: it pumps into the aorta, to the whole body (systemic circulation); the right only to the nearby lungs (pulmonary circulation).

## 3. Myocardium
- Cardiac muscle (review from the previous course): **striated** (sarcomeres; striation **weaker** than skeletal muscle: fewer contractile components); **1 or 2 central nuclei**; many **mitochondria** (EM; [Slides] up to 40% of cell volume); **intercalated discs** (mechanical and electrical coupling); one **fibre = several cells** in a linear, branched arrangement. T tubules transmit depolarization into the cell; sarcoplasmic reticulum stores calcium. [Slides] SR and T tubules form **diads** at Z lines.
- Cells arranged in a **spiral** around the chambers: maximal efficiency in pumping (contraction expels blood; relaxation lets the chamber fill).
- **Highly vascularized**: dense capillaries and small vessels (constant oxygen demand). A student guessed "fibroblasts" for the vessels in the image.
- **Collagen network** (blue with **Masson's trichrome**): small amount, but a **scaffold** that maintains the structure of the ventricles and heart.

## 4. Epicardium, coronary vessels and pericardium
- Epicardium: fibroelastic CT with **adipocytes**, **nerves and ganglia** (nerves = axons only with Schwann cells; ganglia also contain neuronal **somata**), **lymphatic vessels**, and **coronary arteries**; covered by **mesothelium** (simple squamous). [Slides] Same list.
- **Coronary circulation:** the blood in the chambers does not supply the wall; coronary arteries run in the epicardium and branch into the myocardium. **Blocked coronary artery: myocardial infarction (heart attack)** (clinical).
- **Pericardium** (double-walled sac): epicardium (visceral pericardium), **pericardial cavity** with a little **pericardial fluid**, **parietal pericardium** (mesothelium + loose CT). Function: prevents **friction** between the beating heart and surrounding organs. **Pericarditis** (clinical): excess fluid from inflammation or infection constrains contraction and relaxation. [Lucía] Also lists the fibrous pericardium.

## 5. Valves and the conducting system
| | Atrioventricular valves | Semilunar valves |
|---|---|---|
| Which | Tricuspid (right), mitral (left) | Pulmonary, aortic |
| Cusp structure | **Core of dense connective tissue lined by endothelium** (both sides) | Same |
| **Chordae tendineae** | **Yes**: cords of dense CT lined by endothelium, binding the cusps to the **papillary muscles**; **prevent regurgitation** during **systole** | **No** |

- [Slides] Fibrous rings at the base of the AV valves; insertion of a chorda tendinea in a papillary muscle (see the slide).
- **Intrinsic regulation of heart rate:**

| Component | Location / features |
|---|---|
| **Sinoatrial node** | Upper **right atrium**; **pacemaker**. [Slides] Small mass of **modified cardiac muscle cells**: fusiform, smaller, fewer myofibrils |
| **Atrioventricular node** | Lower right atrium; similar cells, with **cytoplasmic projections branching** in various directions (network) |
| **AV bundle (of His)** | Along the **interventricular septum**, splits into left and right bundles, branches to both ventricles |
| **Purkinje fibers** | Distal fibers of the bundle, in the **subendocardium**; connect to cardiomyocytes by **gap junctions** |

- Student question: is the impulse carried by nerves between the nodes? **No**: by the intrinsic conducting system. The nodes are innervated and the CNS adjusts the rhythm, but conduction does not depend on nerves.
- **Purkinje fibers vs cardiomyocytes** (to distinguish): Purkinje fibers are in the **subendocardium**; **weaker striation** (fewer myofibrils, [Slides] at the periphery); **larger and more rounded**; rich in **mitochondria and glycogen**; 1-2 central nuclei. They are **modified cardiac muscle cells**.

## 6. The blood vascular system: the vessel wall
- A **closed** system: arteries, microvasculature (**capillaries**: exchange of nutrients, O2, CO2), venules and veins back to the atria.
- All the vessels of the body in a line would go **around the world at least once, probably twice**; about 150,000 million vessels (transcript) **[verify]**. [Lucía] Twice around the world.
- **Artery vs vein** (low magnification): artery has a **thicker muscle layer** and a more **regular, round lumen**; vein: **larger lumen**, **thinner wall**, **irregular** shape.
- **Three tunics** (parallel to the heart's three layers):

| Tunic | Content |
|---|---|
| **Tunica intima** | **Endothelium** + subendothelial CT (present or not, depending on vessel size) |
| **Tunica media** | **Smooth muscle** + extracellular matrix |
| **Tunica adventitia (externa)** | **Connective tissue**; **no epithelium** at the outer surface: it blends with the CT of the organ |

- Boundaries: **internal elastic membrane** (intima/media; better seen in arteries; bright pink with conventional staining) and **external elastic membrane** (media/adventitia; [Slides] only in some arteries).

## 7. Endothelium [Slides]
- Nucleated **polygonal** cells; **basement membrane** on the basal side; developed **cytoskeleton** (intermediate filaments: desmin, vimentin); joined by **tight junctions** (also desmosomes, gap junctions); **abundant pinocytotic vesicles** (**transcytosis**).
- Why transcytosis: the cells are tightly joined, so larger molecules cannot pass between them; nutrients go into tissues and waste back into the blood by transcytosis.
- **Functions:** lining (barrier); **restricts** some macromolecules and **monitors** the exchange of small molecules; **leukocyte extravasation** (an active process when there is damage; [Slides] at capillaries and postcapillary venules); **hormonal/metabolic** activity: growth factors, **nitric oxide** (relaxes the smooth muscle of the media), vasoconstrictor and vasodilator molecules.

## 8. Tunica media and tunica adventitia
- **Media:** concentric layers of **smooth muscle** + ECM (collagen, elastic fibres, proteoglycans). Muscle contraction/relaxation regulates the lumen and wall stiffness: **blood flow and blood pressure**. ECM gives **resiliency, elasticity, cohesion**; the **collagen/elastic ratio** sets how elastic the vessel is (collagen strong and rigid). Student question: the **ECM is made by the smooth muscle cells themselves** (no fibroblasts in the media).
- **Adventitia:** CT blending into the surrounding tissue; stabilizes the vessel's position.
  - **Vasa vasorum**: small vessels in the adventitia of **thick-walled** vessels, supplying the outer media where diffusion from the lumen cannot reach. For similar wall thickness, **veins need more vasa vasorum** (they carry deoxygenated blood).
  - **Nervi vascularis**: **efferent (motor)** nerves controlling smooth muscle contraction (in all vessels with smooth muscle), and **afferent (sensory)** endings sensing e.g. oxygen and pressure.

## 9. Arterial system
| | **Elastic arteries** (conducting) | **Muscular arteries** (distributing) | **Arterioles** (resistance vessels) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Examples / size | **Aorta, pulmonary arteries** and main branches; the largest. [Slides] brachiocephalic trunk, common carotid, subclavian, common iliac | Most of the named arteries; wide range of sizes (large to small) | Smallest; before the capillaries |
| Intima | Endothelium + subendothelial CT with **scattered smooth muscle cells** | Thinner subendothelial CT (absent in small ones); **prominent internal elastic membrane** | Endothelium, scant CT |
| **Media** | **Very thick**: ~**50-70 layers** of smooth muscle separated by **fenestrated elastic lamellae** | Mostly **smooth muscle** (more prominent than ECM); elastic **fibres** but **no lamellae**; collagen. [Slides] muscle in a spiral | **1-2 layers** of smooth muscle |
| Adventitia | CT, **vasa vasorum**, nerves | CT; sometimes external elastic membrane; **nerves always**; vasa vasorum only in larger ones | Thin; nerve endings |
| Function | Absorb the high systolic pressure; turn **pulsatile** flow into **continuous** flow | Distribute blood to organs; with arterioles, **control blood pressure** | Regulate flow into capillaries; **protect capillaries** |

- **Elastic lamellae:** elastin sheets with **fenestrations (pores)** that let nutrients, O2 and waste reach the smooth muscle (the lamellae are otherwise impermeable). Their **number and thickness** change with **blood pressure and age**. [Slides] Facilitate the transfer of stress through the wall.
- **Pulsatile to continuous flow (lecturer's example):** in **systole** the elastic wall distends and stores some blood; in **diastole** (no output from the heart) the **passive recoil** pushes it on: continuous flow. A rigid artery would give flow only in systole.
- Stains: Masson's trichrome (collagen blue); H&E (elastic lamellae bright pink); [Slides] Van Gieson for elastic fibres (blue to black).
- **Metarterioles**: between arterioles and capillaries; smooth muscle only as **incomplete rings** (not a continuous layer). [Slides] Preferential channels to the capillary bed.

## 10. Capillaries
- **Only a tunica intima: endothelium** (with basement membrane); a cross section fits one or two erythrocytes.
- Ideal for exchange: **thin wall** + **slow flow** (set by the arterioles). They form **capillary beds** between the arterial and venous systems.
- **Pericytes**: support cells **embedded in the same basement membrane** as the endothelial cells, scattered (not a continuous layer, not seen in light microscopy); **not smooth muscle**, but **contractile**; take part in **tissue regeneration** (replace damaged endothelial cells).
- **Permeability** depends on the molecule and the physiological state (**inflammation** greatly increases it):

| Molecules | Examples | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Small **lipid-soluble** | O2, CO2, some vitamins ([Slides] steroid hormones) | Diffusion through the cell: **transcellular** pathway |
| Small **water-soluble** | Amino acids, glucose ([Slides] water, ions; 3-6 nm) | Spaces between cells: **paracellular** pathway |
| **Macromolecules** | [Slides] antibodies, albumin, lipoprotein complexes, peptide hormones | Vesicles: **transcytosis** (pinocytosis) |
