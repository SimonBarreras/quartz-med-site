## 1. The biophysics block of IOSI
- After medical physics (imaging), the course turns to the **biophysics of systems**: (1) **musculoskeletal**: bones, ligaments, muscles; (2) **cardiovascular**: properties of **blood** and **vessels**, the **electrical** behaviour and the **elastic** properties of the heart; (3) **respiratory**: how the lungs are inflated without muscles attached to them (elasticity of the lungs and surface-active materials, garbled **[verify]**).
- Elements of the musculoskeletal system:

| Element | Mechanical behaviour (lecturer) |
|---|---|
| **Bone** | **Rigid**: keeps its shape under force, unless the force is very strong |
| **Ligaments** (at joints) | Keep the **relative position** of the bones; **elastic**: can be deformed |
| **Tendons** | Elastic |
| **Muscles** | Elastic and **active**: they **produce force** between bones |

- [Lucía] Elements: bones, ligaments, tendons, muscles; all deformable; simplest response = bones, most complicated = muscles.
- High school mechanics analyses **rigid bodies** that never change shape. **Real systems** are made of chemical compounds held by bonds: they can be **deformed** and **broken**.

## 2. Three videos: when forces exceed the limits
| Video | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Weightlifter** (2008 Olympic Games; about **148 kg**, "more than 1,000 N" along the arm) | The elbow joint failed | **Huge forces** can cross a limit and break or tear elements |
| **Player who stops and changes direction** (foot fixed, body rotating) | Ankle injury: ligament torn or bone broken | The force was **normal** (body weight) but in an **unexpected direction** (rotation): the joint is not designed to resist it |
| **Football player hit on the leg** by another player | Bones of the leg broken | Bones resist forces along the **axial** direction; a **transverse** force, not huge, breaks them |

- Goal of the block: understand the real behaviour of the MSK system; first **statics** (normal behaviour, no fractures: forces, muscle contraction, rotation of a joint), then **elasticity** (deformation of real materials).

## 3. Forces acting on the MSK system
- **Action at a distance:** in practice only **gravity** (body weight, weight of objects). The transcript lists four fundamental interactions (gravity, electrostatic, electromagnetic, nuclear) **[verify]**; "you can neglect everything" except gravity.
- **Contact forces:** need two objects touching, a **contact surface** that transfers force. In a joint, two bones in contact: a contact force.
- **Newton's laws** (to remember): (1) inertia; (2) **total force = mass × acceleration**: a non-zero total force gives an acceleration; (3) **action and reaction**: two bodies in contact exert opposite forces on each other.
- **Muscles:** the force follows the **direction of the fibres**; only **contraction**: a muscle between two bones **pulls on both bones toward each other**; it can never push. [Lucía] Same: only a pulling force, in the direction of its fibres.
- The human body is **far from equilibrium**: muscles must stay active to keep posture (relax them all and you fall).

## 4. Joints: the simplification used
- Joints can allow **rotations** and **displacements**; displacements are rare and out of scope.
- Up to **three axes of rotation** in one joint (example given: a joint that rotates in three directions, garbled); analysed here only with **one axis** and **no displacement**, e.g. **flexion-extension of the elbow**. [Lucía] Same simplification (elbow flexion-extension, ignoring pronation-supination); results apply to more general systems.
- The rotation axis is drawn **perpendicular to the plane** of the drawing.
- **No displacement** because the **ligaments** hold the bones in place: in statics that is their only role.

## 5. The shopping-bag problem
- Christmas shopping example: carry bags with the **elbow flexed and the bags close to the body**, or far from it? Close is better: **the biceps force is smaller**. (Holding bags on the forearm may also impair circulation: physiology, not biophysics.)
- Model: forearm (rigid), elbow joint, biceps attached near the elbow, load in the hand.
- **Load alone** cannot be in equilibrium: it would cause acceleration (rotation downwards).
- Add the **muscle force** (pulling). If these were the only two forces, equilibrium (total force = 0, Newton's second law) would give **muscle force = load** whatever the distance: contradicts experience. So something is missing.
- Weight of the forearm (a student's suggestion): correct but not the missing piece (next session).
- **Missing force: the joint force** (contact force at the elbow, where the two bones touch). **Minimum system for any joint: external force (gravity or an object) + muscular force + joint force.** More forces (several muscles) can be added; only two forces is rare.
- [Lucía] Minimum set = load (FL), muscle force (FM), joint force (Fa). Large Fa can stress and tear the ligament (sprain); large FM can tear muscle or tendon (strain); large FL can break bone.

## 6. Equilibrium and torque (introduction)
- Equilibrium conditions: **no displacement** (total force = 0; ligaments) and **no rotation**.
- What produces rotation? **Torque (moment)**. Door example:
  - Pushing far from the hinges needs a small force; close to the axis, a very large force for the same effect.
  - Pushing **along** the door, toward the hinge, produces **no rotation** (it can only break the hinge).
- **Three ingredients of torque: the force, the distance to the axis of rotation, and the orientation of the force.** Combined into one magnitude (continued the next day).
- [Lucía] Lever law F1·d1 = F2·d2; elbow = **3rd class lever** (muscle close to the hinge, load far); torque in N·m, clockwise negative, anticlockwise positive; equilibrium = total force 0 and total torque 0; worked example (10 kg load, dL = 40 cm, dm = 4 cm: Fm = 1000 N at 90°, about 3000 N at 160°).
