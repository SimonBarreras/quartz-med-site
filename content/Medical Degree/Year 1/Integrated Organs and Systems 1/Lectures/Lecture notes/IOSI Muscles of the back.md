## 1. The mental map
| | **Migrated (extrinsic)** | **Autochthonous (intrinsic)** |
|---|---|---|
| Position | **Superficial** (first found in dissection, after skin, fat, fascia) | **Deep** |
| Attachments | From the **axial skeleton** to the **upper limb** (superficial group) or to the **ribs** (intermediate group) | Start **and** end on the **axial skeleton** (spine, head, ribs) |
| Function | Move the **upper limb** and the **ribs** (breathing) | **Extend, laterally flex and rotate** the spine and the head |
| Innervation | **Anterior rami** of spinal nerves, **except the trapezius (CN XI)** | **Posterior rami** of spinal nerves, **all of them, without exception** |
| Groups | **Superficial** (5 muscles), **intermediate** (2) | **Spinotransverse**, **extensors and rotators** (erector spinae, transversospinalis), **short segmental**, and the **suboccipital** muscles |

- **Why the innervation splits:** a spinal nerve leaves the spine and divides into an **anterior ramus** (goes forward, to the extrinsic muscles, superficial to the intrinsic ones) and a **posterior ramus** (always to the muscles next to the spine: the intrinsic group).
- [Slides] Main actions of the back muscles: extension, lateral flexion and rotation. Origin = the most caudal point. "According to its embryological origin": migrated vs autochthonous.
- Order to study: **superficial to deep**; within the deep group, **long and superficial to short and deep**.

## 2. Superficial group (migrated): spine to upper limb
- Five muscles: **trapezius, latissimus dorsi, levator scapulae, rhomboid minor, rhomboid major**. All **anterior rami, except the trapezius**. Function: move the upper limb. The **spine is the fixed point** (origin); what moves is the scapula, clavicle or humerus.

| Muscle | Origin [Slides] | Insertion [Slides] | Nerve | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trapezius** | Occipital bone (external protuberance, superior nuchal line), nuchal ligament, spinous processes **C7-T12** | **Clavicle** (slides: "lateral 1/2"), **acromion** and **spine of the scapula** | **Accessory nerve (CN XI)**, "the most important"; also anterior rami **C3-C4** | Three parts: **descending, transverse, ascending** fibres. Scapular elevation, retraction and rotation; neck lateral flexion and extension: brings the limb towards the midline |
| **Latissimus dorsi** | Spinous processes **T7-L5**, supraspinous ligament, **thoracolumbar fascia**, ribs **IX-XII**, **iliac crest** | **Intertubercular groove (sulcus) of the humerus**, on its **anterior** aspect (changes plane, posterior to anterior) | **Thoracodorsal nerve** (C6-C8) | Shoulder **adduction**, **internal rotation** and **extension**; retracts the shoulder. "Helps me scratch my back" |
| **Levator scapulae** | **Transverse processes C1-C4** | **Superior (superomedial) angle of the scapula** | **Dorsal scapular nerve** (C5) and anterior rami **C3-C4** | **Elevates the scapula** (the name tells the function); lateral neck flexion |
| **Rhomboid minor** (smaller, **superior**) | Nuchal ligament, spinous processes **C7-T1** | **Medial border of the scapula** | **Dorsal scapular nerve** (C4-C5) | Retraction, rotation and stabilization of the scapula |
| **Rhomboid major** (bigger, **inferior**) | Spinous processes **T2-T5** | **Medial border of the scapula** | **Dorsal scapular nerve** (C4-C5) | Same as rhomboid minor |

- **Clavicle insertion of the trapezius:** the slides give "Clavicle lateral 1/2"; he said only "and also the clavicle". Learn the slide version, since the insertions of this group may be examined.
- **Shrugging the shoulders** uses **all the superficial muscles except latissimus dorsi**: trapezius (CN XI), levator scapulae and both rhomboids (dorsal scapular nerve).

## 3. Intermediate group (migrated): spine to ribs, breathing
| Muscle | Origin [Slides] | Insertion [Slides] | Nerve | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Serratus posterior superior** | Spinous processes **C7-T3**, nuchal ligament | **Superior border of ribs II-IV** | Anterior rami **T1-T4** | **Elevates the ribs**: breathing **in** |
| **Serratus posterior inferior** | Spinous processes **T11-L2** | **Inferior border of ribs IX-XII** | Anterior rami **T9-T12** | **Depresses the ribs**: compresses the thorax, breathing **out** |

- "Serratus" from *sierra* (saw): its saw-toothed shape helps to recognize it.

## 4. Deep group (autochthonous): posterior rami, always
To see them you must first cut the extrinsic muscles. **All innervated by posterior rami** ("If I ask you innervation, tell me posterior rami"). Exact origins and insertions **not examined** (exam flags).

### 4a. Spinotransverse (from spinous to transverse processes)
| Muscle | Course [Slides] | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Splenius capitis** | Nuchal ligament, spinous processes **C7-T3**, to the **mastoid process** of the temporal bone and the **lateral third of the superior nuchal line** (no transverse process in the head, so it goes there instead) | Head extension, lateral flexion, rotation |
| **Splenius cervicis (colli)** | Spinous processes **T3-T6** to the **transverse processes C1-C3** | Neck extension, lateral flexion, rotation |

- Difference between them: **where they end** (head vs cervical transverse processes). [Slides] "Wrapping" the deeper muscles in the head and neck. Nerve: [Slides] middle and inferior cervical nerves (posterior rami).

### 4b. Extensors and rotators: erector spinae (superficial, the longest)
- Three muscles, **lateral to medial: iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis**. Each has **three parts**. They **act as one**: **extension and lateral flexion** of the spine.

| Muscle | Position | Parts | Course (general) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iliocostalis** | **Most lateral** | Lumborum, thoracis, cervicis | From the **iliac crest** (and sacrum) to the **ribs** ("ilio-costal") |
| **Longissimus** | **Intermediate** | **Thoracis** (not lumborum), cervicis, **capitis** (reaches the mastoid process) | To the lateral aspect of the spine (transverse processes), a few segments above |
| **Spinalis** | **Most medial**, next to the spinous processes | Thoracis, cervicis, capitis ([Slides] capitis inconstant) | Spinous to spinous processes |

- [Slides] Iliocostalis nerve supply "posterior rami cervical nerves": he said posterior rami for the whole group.

### 4c. Extensors and rotators: transversospinalis (deeper, shorter)
- From **transverse** processes up to **spinous** processes. Semispinalis: **extension and rotation** ("instead of doing lateral flexion they do rotation"); multifidus and rotatores: see the table. Superficial to deep:

| Muscle | Spans | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Semispinalis** (thoracis, cervicis, capitis) | **4-6 segments** | Most superficial of the three. [Slides] from transverse processes C4-T12; capitis to the occipital between the nuchal lines |
| **Multifidus** | **2-4 segments** | Starts lower than semispinalis (sacrum, ilium: [Slides]). Extension, rotation, lateral flexion |
| **Rotatores** (lumborum, thoracis, cervicis) | **1-2 segments** | From transverse processes, 1-2 levels up: **long** rotatores to the **spinous process**, **short** ones to the **lamina** ([Slides] same). Spine rotation |

- **Rule: the deeper, the shorter.** The segment counts are in the **Osmosis video**, not the slides; he said he would send the figure.

### 4d. Short segmental muscles (shortest, deepest)
| Muscle | Between | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Interspinales** | Two adjacent **spinous** processes | Bring them together: **extension** |
| **Intertransversarii** | Two adjacent **transverse** processes ([Slides] cervical and lumbar) | **Lateral flexion** |
| **Levatores costarum** | **Transverse process** (C7-T11) to the **superior border of the ribs** (I-XII) [Slides] | **Elevate the ribs** |

- **Two rib elevators from different groups** (flagged): **serratus posterior superior** (migrated, intermediate group, anterior rami) and **levatores costarum** (autochthonous, posterior rami).

## 5. Suboccipital muscles
- Different region, as the C1-C2 joints were different ([[IOSI Joints of the spine and ribs]]). Four muscles, **all innervated by the suboccipital nerve (posterior ramus of C1)**. **Origins and insertions: study them.**

| Muscle | Lower attachment | Upper attachment | Action [Slides] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rectus capitis posterior major** | **C2** (spinous process of the axis) | Head: **inferior nuchal line** | Head extension and rotation |
| **Rectus capitis posterior minor** (medial to the major) | **C1** (posterior tubercle of the atlas) | Head: inferior nuchal line, medial part | Head extension |
| **Obliquus capitis superior** | **C1** (transverse process of the atlas) | Head: occipital bone | Head extension and lateral flexion |
| **Obliquus capitis inferior** | **C2** (spinous process of the axis) | **C1** (transverse process of the atlas): the only one not reaching the head | Head rotation |

- His summary: "Rectus capitis major C2, minor C1, obliquus superior head-C1, obliquus inferior C1-C2."
- **[check]** The slide for rectus capitis posterior major says "Axis spinous p (C1)": the axis is **C2**, and he said C2.
- **Suboccipital triangle**: formed by **three** of them: **obliquus superior, obliquus inferior, rectus capitis posterior major**. Contents: **vertebral artery** and **suboccipital nerve (C1)** ([Slides] "posterior branch of C1").
- **Clinical:** detaching these muscles in surgery and failing to reattach them causes **loss of head rotation**.

## 6. Clinical: the lumbar spine approach
- Layers: skin, fat, fascia, extrinsic muscles, intrinsic muscles, bone. In surgery the muscles are **treated as one block**: cut the fascia and separate all of them together from the spine (held apart by an "automatic instrument") down to the bone, exposing **spinous processes, laminae, facets and transverse processes**. Example shown: a patient operated for **scoliosis**. [Slides] "Lumbar spine approach" (images, pages 42-47).
- When studying, though, think in layers.
