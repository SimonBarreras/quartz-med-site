## 1. Course framing
- IOS is part of the integrated curriculum: after fundamentals of morphology, now integrated organ systems, later medical-surgical subjects.
- [Notes] Teaching guide: 12 ECTS, 2nd trimester; units: cardiovascular, musculoskeletal, respiratory, nephro-urinary. Musculoskeletal programme: statics/forces; macroscopic and radiological anatomy of the vertebral column; of the shoulder girdle and upper limb; of the pelvis and lower limb.
- Coordinator: the system is hard because it is very three-dimensional and mixes function, biophysics and biology; the schedule progresses in detail.

## 2. Number of bones; functions
- A newborn has about **300** bones, an adult **206**: bones **fuse** during growth.
- 206 is an approximation (people have more or fewer depending on fusion); no need to memorize.
- **Osteology:** the study of bones and the skeletal system.
- **Functions of the skeleton:** support, movement, protection, and **blood cell production**: a living organ, not just a scaffold.
- Clinical image: X-ray of an arm with movement in the middle of the arm: a **fracture of the humerus**. First know what is normal, then diagnose and treat.

## 3. Axial and appendicular skeleton
| | Axial | Appendicular |
|---|---|---|
| Parts | Mostly head, neck and trunk | Free limbs, articulating with the axial skeleton |
| Main function | **Protection** (skull: brain; ribs and spine: lungs, heart, spinal cord) | **Movement** |
| Origin | Transcript "stereotone" **[verify]** | "Mesoderm" |

- [Notes] Axial: skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum. Appendicular: bones of the upper and lower extremities.
- Example: the proximal humerus shown as a long bone ("the femur is always the first answer, but it's not").

## 4. Bone as a living tissue
- Composition: cells and extracellular matrix; the matrix has **organic** and **inorganic** components.
- **Always remodelling**: building and removing bone, even while sitting.
- **Metabolic function**: regulates minerals; makes blood cells (**haematopoiesis**).
- Cells (reference slide, not to memorize): **osteogenic cells** form **osteoblasts**; osteoblasts **create bone**; **osteocytes** are osteoblasts inside the matrix; **osteoclasts** destroy bone (transcript "osteoblasts that destroy bone"). Together: the remodelling cycle.
- **Inorganic component**: the biggest part of the matrix, **60-70% minerals**: gives **strength**. **Organic matrix**: mostly **collagen I**: gives **flexibility**. Bone is rigid but also flexible.

## 5. Cortical and cancellous bone; marrow; periosteum; endosteum; cartilage
- Bone cut in half: **cortical (compact)** bone outside, gives strength; **cancellous (spongy)** bone inside, containing the **bone marrow**.
- **Marrow:** mostly **yellow**; **red** marrow in some bones, e.g. **flat bones** and the ends of the femur and humerus. Red marrow makes blood cells.
- **Periosteum:** connective tissue covering bone.
  - Very **innervated**: the pain when you hit or break a bone. Example: the **tibia**, the most exposed bone, only skin over its anterior aspect.
  - Allows growth in **thickness** and **repairs bone**. Clinical images: a wrist fracture fixed with metal **pins**, with new bone formed through the periosteum; an angulated fracture, the same patient weeks later with new bone, and healed after months.
- **Endosteum:** connective tissue inside the cortical bone, surrounding the spongy bone; contains **osteoprogenitor cells** that become osteoblasts.
  - [Lucía] Periosteum: outer fibrous layer and inner osteoprogenitor layer; Sharpey fibres attach it; covers bone except at synovial articulations and muscle attachments.
- **Articular cartilage:** **hyaline cartilage** covers articular surfaces to allow movement. In the cadaver-lab bones it is gone (flat surfaces without cartilage); in the cadaver it is present. Clinical video: elbow with a **radial head** fracture, the radial head covered by cartilage; not repairable, so a **prosthesis**; cartilage is essential for soft movement without pain.

## 6. Vascularization
- Main vessels are **diaphyseal**: the **nutrient (diaphyseal) artery and vein** in the long part of the bone.
- Also vessels in the **epiphysis** and **metaphysis**; with age the diaphyseal vessels are the most important.

## 7. Growth and ossification
- Two hip radiographs: the **child's** is recognized because the bones are **not fused**.

| | Endochondral ossification | Intramembranous ossification |
|---|---|---|
| Importance | **"The important one"**; most bones | Flat bones |
| Model | **Indirect**: a **cartilage** model converts into bone | **No cartilage model**: directly from mesenchymal cells into bone |
| Timing | Begins towards the end of the embryonic period | |
| Examples | | **Skull**, **clavicle** |

- **Epiphyseal plate (growth cartilage):** lines between unfused bones, **only in children**; they disappear when bones fuse. On a child's X-ray these lines are **not fractures**.
- **Ossification nuclei (centres):** the **primary** appears first in the longest part of the bone (transcript "in the earth" **[verify]**); **secondary** nuclei appear later; cartilage converts to bone until, when everything is fused, there is one long bone. Ossification centres look separate on X-ray because cartilage is not seen.
- **Bone age:** newborn hand X-ray: no carpal bones (all cartilage); a 7-year-old's X-ray shows some. Knowing when these bones appear gives the **age of the child**.

## 8. Types of bones (by shape)
| Type | Definition (lecturer) | Examples | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long** | One dimension predominates over the other two | Humerus, femur, tibia, fibula, radius, ulna | Movement |
| **Short** | | **Carpal and tarsal** bones (hands and feet) | |
| **Flat** | Two layers of compact bone with spongy bone inside (in the skull the spongy bone = **diploe**) | Skull, scapula, ribs, clavicle | **Protection** |
| **Irregular** | Many prominences, holes | **Vertebrae** (cervical, thoracic, lumbar), **coxal bone** (ilium + ischium + pubis) | |
| **Pneumatic** | Bones with **air** inside | Skull: frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, temporal, maxilla (sinuses) | |
| **Sesamoid** | Always **within tendons**; act as a **pulley** | **Patella** (the most important example) | Leg extension is harder without the patella |

- A foramen = a **hole**.

## 9. Bone features (reliefs) and parts
- Learn the landmarks of each bone step by step; "it's easier than you think".
- **Distal humerus:** two articular surfaces: **condyle** (transcript "condyne") laterally and **trochlea** medially. **Lateral epicondyle** (palpable at the lateral elbow): insertion of muscles and ligaments. Medially the **epitrochlea** (medial epicondyle): insertion of the muscles that flex the fingers and wrist, and of ligaments.
- **Proximal humerus:** **head**, **neck**, **lesser tuberosity**, **greater tuberosity**, and the **groove** between them.
- Other examples: heads of the humerus and femur, **greater and lesser trochanter** of the femur, the **malleoli** at the ankle.
- **Vertebra:** **spinous process**, **transverse process**, articular **facets**. Holes in the transverse processes (for the **vertebral artery**) mean a **cervical vertebra**; the big central hole is for the spinal cord or spinal nerves.
- **Fossa** (e.g. scapula); many other projections and depressions: know they exist, study bone by bone.
- **Parts of a long bone** (humerus example): **diaphysis** = middle, longest section; **two epiphyses**, proximal and distal. Proximally the humerus articulates with the **scapula**; at the elbow with the **radius and ulna**. Closing list adds **physis** and **metaphysis**.
